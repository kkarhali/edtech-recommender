## 5.0 What this chapter establishes

This chapter establishes what the phase 2 experiment measured, and it states the answer plainly
rather than favourably: under the corrected cold-start condition the hybrid recommender beats
item-based collaborative filtering (CF) on new learners and loses to content-based filtering (CBF)
on the same learners, so the cold-start half of the frozen hypothesis is refuted against one
baseline and upheld only against the other, while the warm-user half holds. It serves research
questions RQ0, RQ1, RQ3 and RQ4 of chapter 1 and judges all six pre-registered success criteria
C1 to C6 exactly as `plan.md` worded them before any phase 2 run. It takes from chapter 3 the

### 5.1.2 The population these numbers describe

### 5.1.3 How to read a Precision@10 here

Three properties of the metric were fixed in chapter 3 before the run and govern every reading below.

First, Precision@10 is ceiling-bound: a learner with fewer than ten held-out items cannot reach 1.0, 45.6% of EEE_2014J's evaluated learners are ceiling-bound, and the share runs from 14.4% (FFF_2013B) to 81.3% (GGG_2014B). <!-- C5.11 -->
On the cold-start slice the attainable ceiling is reported per presentation and runs from 0.6482 (GGG_2014B) to 0.9435 (FFF_2013B), so a cold-start Precision@10 of 0.03 to 0.08 is read against that ceiling and is not by itself evidence that a model failed. <!-- C5.12 -->
This was pre-declared in the protocol, not decided after seeing the values.

Second, a difference **between presentations** is never read as a difference in model quality, because the presentations differ in catalogue size, in test-set sizes and therefore in ceiling. Comparisons are always within a presentation, and the 22 within-presentation comparisons are then aggregated.

Third, "relative" and "percentage points" are distinguished in every sentence.
A relative gain of +31.20% against a baseline whose Precision@10 is 0.1562 is an absolute difference of about +0.0413, not of 31 percentage points, and both forms are given wherever the distinction can be misread. <!-- C5.14 -->

### 5.1.4 Module D: the behaviour of the evaluated population, and what RQ1 can be answered from it

This subsection reports Module D, the descriptive behaviour analysis that research question RQ1 rests on. It is placed here, beside the population description of 5.1.2, because it describes the same learners that every later number is measured on, and because its central caveat is a caveat about reading: these features are computed in hindsight. **Table 5.13** gives the essential rows; the full 22-row tables are in `thesis/tables/table_module_d_behaviour.md`.

Four properties of Module D govern how far anything below may be taken, and all four are read from the code rather than assumed.

- **It is descriptive, and it decides no criterion.** Module D appears in no threshold of the frozen criteria block. Of the modules under `code/`, only the evaluation module imports it; the scoring path — the models, the blend, the cell runner and the feature builder — does not, no segment label or behavioural feature of Module D is an input to any compared model, and a unit test enforces that separation. <!-- C5.128 -->
- **Its features have no time bound.** They are computed from the evaluated learners' rows over the whole presentation rather than from history before the train/test cut, as the module's own docstring states. They therefore describe learners in hindsight and **could not serve as a cold-start prior without leaking the test period** — which is the concrete reason RQ2, as the plan words it, is not answerable here (section 5.11, limitation 7).
- **k = 4 was fixed, not selected.** The number of segments is the module's default, recorded in the code with the note that the plan does not name k; there is no search over k and no model-selection criterion, so the silhouette values below describe the quality of one k = 4 fit rather than the winner of a comparison. <!-- C5.124 -->
- **It ran inside the receipted main run.** Unlike the D1 and D2 diagnostics of section 5.8, Module D was part of the reviewed code and executed once with the main run under its receipt, so it is not a post-hoc second look at the test split. It still revises nothing.

**The population.** Module D describes the evaluated cohort of each presentation — the learners left after the at-least-5-distinct-items and at-least-3-active-days filters — 27,212 learners in total, from 347 (AAA_2014J) to 2,131 (CCC_2014J), with a median of 1,222 per presentation; the cohort size it reports equals `foundation_<pres>.json#counts.evaluated` in 22 of 22 presentations. <!-- C5.119 -->

**The features do separate learners, and widely.** Taking each presentation's own median, click volume runs from 389.0 to 2,475.0 across the 22 presentations, distinct items from 34.0 to 115.0 and active days from 30.0 to 81.0. <!-- C5.121 -->
Within a typical presentation the spread is wider still: the ratio of the median 95th percentile to the median 5th percentile is 42.8 for click volume, 23.6 for active days, 12.5 for distinct first-opening days, 8.1 for distinct items, 5.4 for the mean gap between new items and 4.3 for clicks per active day. <!-- C5.120 -->
These are ratios of medians over the 22 presentations, not a pooled learner distribution, which the artifact does not store.

**The segmentation.** The k = 4 fit returned four non-empty segments in 22 of 22 presentations, the smallest cohort being 347 learners, so the cluster count was never bound by cohort size. <!-- C5.122 -->
Its separation is weak to moderate and the number is reported rather than characterised: the silhouette coefficient has a median of 0.3274 over the 22 presentations, a range of 0.2782 (BBB_2014B) to 0.3614 (FFF_2013J) and quartiles of 0.3139 and 0.3444, with 18 of the 22 values in the 0.30 to 0.40 band and 4 below 0.30 (BBB_2013B, BBB_2013J, BBB_2014B, CCC_2014J). <!-- C5.123 -->
In the 15 presentations with more than 1,000 evaluated learners the coefficient is an estimate computed on a seeded 1,000-learner subsample rather than on the whole cohort, and k was fixed at 4 rather than chosen, so these values say what quality the four-way split has and not that four is the right number of segments. <!-- C5.124 -->
The segments are uneven in size: the largest holds a median 41.3% of a presentation's cohort and the smallest a median 11.2%. <!-- C5.125 -->

**What the segments separate on is how much a learner did.** Ordering each presentation's four segments by mean click volume, the rank-1 segment holds a median 41.0% of the cohort at a median click volume of 2,365.8, against 706.6 at rank 2, 297.2 at rank 3 and 126.8 at rank 4. <!-- C5.126 -->
That ordering carries the other volume features with it but not the rate features: across the 22 presentations the ordering is monotonic for distinct items in 18, for distinct first-opening days in 15 and for active days in 14, while it is monotonic for clicks per active day in only 4 and for the mean gap between new items in none. <!-- C5.127 -->
So the dominant axis of the four segments is activity volume, and the two shape features do not follow it.

**What RQ1 can and cannot be answered from this.** RQ1 asks which observable behaviours separate learners well enough to carry recommendation signal, and Module D answers the first half only, and only for three of the four behaviour families the question names. The first half is answered descriptively and affirmatively for click volume, active-day count and day-ordered sequence — the last represented only by two scalar summaries, the count of distinct first-opening days and the mean gap between new items, and not by any ordered-sequence model (`code/behaviour.py`) <!-- C5.132 -->: click volume, distinct items, active days and distinct first-opening days separate the evaluated learners widely and yield a four-way segmentation in every presentation, of weak-to-moderate quality (silhouette median 0.3274) whose leading axis is activity volume. **Activity type, the fourth family RQ1 names, was not computed as a learner-level Module D feature.** `code/behaviour.py`'s six features are click volume, distinct items, active days, distinct first-opening days, the mean gap between new items and clicks per active day; none encodes the type of activity a learner engaged in. `activity_type` appears elsewhere in this thesis only as a content-side input to the content-based model's item vectors (chapter 3, section 3.6) and as the axis of the intra-list diversity metric (chapter 3, section 3.7) — never as a learner-level behaviour — so the descriptive half of RQ1 covers click volume, active days and day ordering only. <!-- C5.130 --> The second half is not answered here at all, because no Module D output reaches any scored model. The only measured link between behavioural features and ranking quality anywhere in this thesis is the D2 ablation of section 5.8.2, which removes three learner-activity columns from the blend's own feature set and costs a median 0.65% relative Precision@10 on warm users and nothing detectable on cold start; that measures the blend's weighting use of three columns, not a behavioural recommender component, and it is post-hoc. <!-- C5.129 -->
RQ1 is therefore answered descriptively for three of its four named families in its first half, left unexamined for the fourth (activity type), and left unestablished in its second regardless of family; section 6.1 states it in those terms.

The pre-registered test governs the verdict, and the disagreement has a stated cause rather than a rhetorical one: the hybrid wins 16 of 22 presentations with large positive magnitudes (up to +81.90%) while its 6 losses are mostly small, and the sign test discards magnitude while the Wilcoxon uses it. <!-- C5.20 -->
Read together, the warm-user result is a clear win in magnitude and a thinner one in count.

## 5.4 The central finding

Stated plainly, in the direction the artifacts give it and with both baselines named:

> Under the corrected, receipted cold-start condition the hybrid recommender **beats item-based collaborative filtering** on new learners, by a median 7.32% relative over the 22 module presentations, with 17 wins, 3 ties and 2 losses and a Holm-corrected exact Wilcoxon p of 0.000241, **and loses to content-based filtering** on the same learners, by a median 30.28% relative, with 2 wins and 20 losses and the same Holm-corrected p of 0.000241. <!-- C5.31 -->

Because criterion C2 requires the hybrid to beat **both** baselines, C2 is not met, and it fails on
the threshold itself rather than on significance: no amount of statistical power changes a median
below zero. C3 fails through the same comparison in per-presentation form, passing all four conjuncts in only 2 of 22 presentations. <!-- C5.117 -->
The one direction the phase 1 pilot never tested — against
collaborative filtering — is the direction that now holds; the one direction it did test, and
reported as a decisive win, is the direction that now fails. Nothing in this chapter softens that,
and section 5.5 records it as the criterion verdict rather than as a discussion point.

The surviving half of that sentence must not be read as a cold-start success. Section 5.3.2 shows that on this slice collaborative filtering is itself at or below a random ranker and that the hybrid is indistinguishable from that floor, so beating collaborative filtering here is beating a comparator that does not clear chance, and the only model clearly above chance on new learners is content-based filtering — the one the hybrid loses to. <!-- C5.114 -->
The honest one-sentence form of the central finding is therefore: on new learners the hybrid is not distinguishable from a random ranker, it is beaten by content-based filtering, and it beats a collaborative baseline that is itself below the floor.

On warm users the picture is the opposite and C1 is met as written, the hybrid clearing both baselines by a median +31.20% relative against content-based filtering and +10.68% against collaborative filtering, both Holm-corrected significant. <!-- C5.39 -->
The hypothesis is therefore **partially
supported**: its warm-user half holds, its cold-start half is refuted against content-based
filtering and upheld only against collaborative filtering, and its behavioural-signal clause is
unchecked. That is a complete, evidenced, terminal result and is reported as one.

## 5.7 Iteration history, and the cause of each change

Every change to the design, the code or the reported numbers is listed here with the cause that produced it, including the ones that were wrong. Nothing was dropped from the record after being fixed.

### 5.7.1 Phase 1, the pilot (prior evidence, no receipts)

The pilot ran two blend-weight iterations, both reported. Iteration 1 used a fixed two-tier weight (0.6 on collaborative filtering above three visible items, else 0.2) and did not meet the pilot's criterion 1: it beat content-based filtering by +263% relative on warm users but lost to collaborative filtering by −19.9%, because content-based filtering's own Precision@10 (0.118) was far below collaborative filtering's (0.535), so a 40% weight on the weaker component dragged the blend down. <!-- C5.54 -->
Iteration 2 replaced the step function with `alpha = min(0.9, 0.2 + 0.05 * n_visible_items)`, one function changed on the same data and split, and closed the warm-user gap to collaborative filtering from −19.9% to −2.7% relative while the margin over content-based filtering held; the pilot stopped there rather than tuning until the number crossed zero. <!-- C5.55 -->
The pilot's first 22-presentation pass then surfaced a data-quality bug rather than noise: in BBB_2013B, DDD_2013B and DDD_2013J the hybrid's Precision@10 equalled content-based filtering's bit for bit, because `week_from` and `week_to` are missing for 100% of items in exactly those three presentations, a median over an all-missing column returned `NaN`, and the `NaN` propagated through the feature vectors into every content-based and hybrid score; the mean relative gain against collaborative filtering was thereby dragged to −14.8% against a median of −2.9% for the other 19. <!-- C5.56 -->
It was fixed by falling back to 0.0 for an all-missing week column with a safety net on the assembled feature matrix, and all 22 presentations were re-run. <!-- C5.56 -->
The pre-fix numbers stay in the record with their root cause. The hand-picked weight of iteration 2 is the reason criterion C4 exists in phase 2: anything fitted after test scores are seen is exploratory.

### 5.7.2 Phase 2, the methodology (three revisions, all at gate G3)

The protocol needed three revisions and four gate cycles before it passed, and each revision was caused by a measured defect rather than by a reviewer's preference. Revision 1 answered three integrity findings: a ceiling table computed without the protocol's own tie rule (38.1% of EEE_2014J learners hold fewer than ten test items under the rule, not 47.9%), a blend-weight specification that could not be rebuilt by a second implementer, and a refit cold-start condition that removed the held-out learners from the similarity matrix only, leaving popularity counts, content normalisers and the blend's own fitting rows unstated. <!-- C5.57 -->
Revision 2 answered two more: the learner filter and the split rule disagreed, so 106 of 27,212 learners passed the stated filter and still had no validation set while the published tables asserted a zero exclusion count; and the candidate exclusion set encoded the learner's future, which a reviewer probe measured as inflating cold-start Precision@10 on EEE_2014J from 0.0255 to 0.2157 for the popularity baseline and from 0.0333 to 0.1946 for collaborative filtering. <!-- C5.58 -->
Revision 3 answered the repair's own overshoot: applying the warm exclusion set at the validation boundary put the ground truth inside its own exclusion set and scored validation Precision@10 at 0.0000 against 0.1883, which would have made the blend weight, the neighbourhood size and the regulariser degenerate; and click totals were summed with no date bound, so on EEE_2014J 17.8% of warm training-pair clicks post-dated the test boundary. <!-- C5.59 -->
The final rule bounds the test cut at the learner's third distinct day, which makes three active days sufficient as well as necessary: zero learners with an empty slice and zero split-assertion violations across all 27,212 evaluated learners. <!-- C5.60 -->
Two earlier rules were withdrawn with their measurements rather than quietly replaced, the tie-extension rule that emptied train and validation for 1,345 of 28,209 learners and the unbounded day-boundary rule described above. <!-- C5.61 -->

### 5.7.3 Phase 2, the code (three revisions, all at gate G4)

The code needed three revisions and five blockers, and every one was found by executing the code rather than by reading it. Two blockers aborted every run before any result existed: feature rows were subtracted by position rather than by learner id, so the secondary split's arrays differed in length, and the test-scoring counter keyed a scoring without its split name, so one cell scored across five splits was over-counted and the scored-once assertion failed at the end of a run that had completed. <!-- C5.62 -->
Two more, found when a reviewer executed the code for the first time, also aborted on the first cell: an argument passed twice to the leakage check, and a feature attribute read from an object that does not carry it. <!-- C5.63 -->
The fifth was a design question the engineer was not allowed to settle alone: on the tertiary split the two "reads only its allowed rows" assertions fire non-zero by design, 54,193 of 180,925 rows on AAA_2013J, because that split defines no training bound at all; the methodology then ruled those two assertions `not_applicable` on that split only, exactly as two others already were, and they remain must-be-zero on the primary and secondary splits and in every cold-start condition. <!-- C5.64 -->
After the third revision the pipeline completed all 34 cells of a presentation with 305 assertion log lines and zero failures. <!-- C5.65 -->

### 5.7.4 Phase 2, the runs and the reported numbers

The two receipted stages needed no debug round: every command, including the unit tests, the four entry-point refusal cases, the canary and both stages, exited as expected on the first attempt, so 0 of the 3 budgeted debug rounds were used. <!-- C5.66 -->
The reported numbers, by contrast, needed one revision. The run gate's first cycle returned three blockers against `results/summary.md` alone, none of which changed a result: one wrong number (a cold-start median printed as 0.0473 where the artifact holds 0.052862, which hid an 11.6% margin behind an apparent tie), one label the artifact contradicted (a sign reversal marked on a cell the artifact records as not reversed), and the absence of the OULAD population limitation that the frozen idea file requires that file to carry. <!-- C5.67 -->

After the criteria were closed, two diagnostics were added and run with the candidate's approval. Their results are section 5.8. ## 5.9 Comparison with the literature, under chapter 2's caveats

Chapter 2 fixed the caveat that governs this section: none of the reported numbers in the reviewed literature is comparable with a Precision@10 on OULAD, because the tasks, the metrics, the cut-offs and the domains differ, and several of the proposal's high-scoring papers report accuracy of a classification rather than a ranking at all. The comparison below is therefore a comparison of **directions and designs**, and every number is labelled with its own task.

The warm-user hybrid median of 0.1720 Precision@10 and the cold-start median of 0.0473 are not comparable with the Precision@5 figures the literature reports, because K differs, the candidate set differs, the domain differs and, in two of the closest cases, the samples are 500 crawled learners and one private platform's logs. <!-- C5.86 -->
What is comparable is direction. The closest analogue in the sources opened, a course hybrid built from session logs of an online platform, reported collaborative filtering as its best model at Precision@5 of 0.4, with content-based strategies not yielding significant results but helping mitigate cold start; on the warm slice here collaborative filtering is likewise the stronger single baseline (median 0.1707 against content-based filtering's 0.1562), while on the cold-start slice the direction inverts and content-based filtering is far the stronger (median 0.0728 against 0.0377). <!-- C5.87 -->
The literature also already contains hybrids that lose to a single technique: the proposal's own behavioural-profile hybrid reports F1 of 0.839 against pure collaborative filtering's 0.923, its advantage being computation time, and an adaptive recommender that is best on accuracy, recall and F1 ranks last of twelve on precision. <!-- C5.88 -->
So the result reported here — a hybrid that beats both single techniques on engaged learners and loses to one of them on new learners — is not anomalous in this literature; it is the kind of outcome chapter 2 warned the design to be able to detect, which is why a popularity baseline, a random floor and a significance test were required before any claim. <!-- C5.89 -->
A reproducibility study in which 11 of 12 neural approaches were outperformed by well-tuned simple methods is the same warning from the other side, and it is why nothing here is compared against a deep model that was not run. <!-- C5.90 -->

Against the gap chapter 2 identified — the scarcity of behaviour-aware hybrid recommenders evaluated with a ranking metric on public learner clickstream data, with an explicit cold-start slice and replication across course presentations — this chapter supplies exactly that evidence, and the evidence is partly negative. The gap is filled by the measurement, not by the direction of the outcome.

## 5.10 Threats to validity

The threats register is `methodology/threats_to_validity.md`, with 29 rows, each carrying either a mitigation with its protocol section or a residual limitation in the words used here. The residuals that bear on how the results above may be read are these.

**Internal validity.** The primary split is time-ordered strictly within each learner but not across the cohort, so the similarity matrix, the popularity counts and the blend model are fitted on other learners' pairs that can post-date a given learner's test items; the secondary split removes this at the cost of dropping late-joining learners, and it is the split that reverses the warm-versus-CBF sign reported in section 5.6.2. <!-- C5.91 -->
The cold-start cohort is a seeded 20% draw stratified by quintile of training-pair count, so it matches the warm cohort's activity distribution but is not a real new registration; the literal latest-arriving construction was run as a robustness check, and each cold-start claim here rests on the stratified draw. <!-- C5.92 -->
The blend model is refitted per presentation, per condition and per k, so it is never extrapolating outside the visible-history range it was fitted in, which is what makes the cold-start result a statement about the architecture rather than about a mis-specified blend; if the refitted model still differed sharply from the warm one, that reading would weaken, and D1's alpha distributions are what a reader should check it against. <!-- C5.93 -->

**Construct validity.** Clicks are a proxy for learning need, engagement is reported as engagement and never as attainment, and no causal claim about learning, retention or attainment follows from any number in this chapter. The hypothesis names dwell time, which the dataset does not record anywhere, so the behavioural signal is thinner than the hypothesis describes and the phrase "dwell time" is used for nothing that was measured. <!-- C5.94 -->
Unobserved items are counted as negatives although exposure is missing-not-at-random, so an item a learner never opened may be an unobserved positive; this is unmodelled. Precision@10 is ceiling-bound for 14.4% to 81.3% of evaluated learners depending on the presentation, and the tie-break that decides the tenth place is arbitrary though pre-registered and identical across models, so absolute cold-start values carry a component that is an artefact of resource numbering while comparisons do not. <!-- C5.95 -->
Excluding already-seen items defines a task that is not next-click prediction, so these numbers are not comparable with sequence papers that include seen items.

**Conclusion validity.** The 22 presentations are 7 modules, so the across-22 tests are indicative of generalisation and not a random sample of courses. A statistically detectable gap can be practically negligible, which is why an effect size accompanies every p-value above. The four families of comparisons were declared before the run and Holm correction applied within each; the exploratory family supports no criterion and none of its numbers is promoted to evidence for one after the fact. <!-- C5.96 -->
The added baselines and metrics beyond the plan's four models are recorded deviations and decide nothing, and the criterion verdicts of section 5.5 are kept visually separate from them so that no favourable added metric can be mistaken for a criterion that passed. <!-- C5.97 -->
One pre-registered outcome did not occur but remains part of how these criteria must be read: below an effective n of 6 the exact two-sided test cannot reach p < 0.05, in which case a criterion's test conjunct would be recorded `unchecked` rather than failed; the effective n here was 22, 22, 22 and 21, so no conjunct ended `unchecked` for want of pairs. <!-- C5.98 -->

## 5.11 Limitations

Each limitation is stated with the number that bounds it. Together they are the scope of every claim in this chapter.

1. **The evaluated population is narrowed twice, towards engaged learners.** Two pre-registered filters — at least five distinct resources and at least three distinct active days — remove 2,016 of the 29,228 learners who appear in the logs, and the second removes precisely the one- and two-visit learners closest to the frozen idea file's "actual new users": the 997 learners excluded by it hold a mean of 10.0 and a median of 8 distinct resources over a mean of 2.66 and a median of 2 active days, against 55 resources over 52 days for the 27,212 retained. <!-- C5.99 -->
2. **Cold start here is simulated, not observed.** The cold-start slice is built by truncating an evaluated learner's visible history to k items and refitting every fitted quantity without that learner, not by measuring an actual new-user population; the learners whose whole recorded activity falls on one or two days are the ones the filters removed. No result in this chapter speaks for them. <!-- C5.100 -->
3. **The dataset is one institution's, and it records no dwell time.** OULAD is a UK Open University distance-learning cohort of 32,593 learner rows counted in `studentInfo.csv`, not the candidate's own institution, so nothing here licenses a deployment decision elsewhere without re-validation; and its interaction table carries only a day offset and a click count, with no duration field anywhere. <!-- C5.101 -->
4. **The replication unit is not 22 independent draws.** The 22 presentations are 7 modules, two to four presentations each, sharing catalogues and syllabi, so the across-22 tests are indicative of generalisation rather than a random sample of courses, and the per-module breakdown of section 5.6.1 is the check a reader should apply. <!-- C5.102 -->
5. **Cold-start Precision@10 is small by nature and must be read against its ceiling.** The hybrid's cold-start values run from 0.0295 to 0.0779 with a median of 0.0473, and the popularity baseline's from 0.0120 to 0.0484, against a per-presentation attainable ceiling of 0.6482 to 0.9435; on EEE_2014J at k = 2, 81% of cold-start learners score exactly 0, the per-learner standard deviation is 0.059 and the standard error of the mean is 0.0041, about 16% of the mean, rising to roughly 28% at the smallest cohort of 69 learners. <!-- C5.103 -->
   C3's per-presentation floors are therefore judged on a noisy quantity, which is the frozen criterion rather than a design defect, and whether those floors should stay per-presentation is an open question for the candidate.
   The exploratory comparison of section 5.3.2 bounds the same point from below: on this slice only content-based filtering is clearly above a random ranker (+0.0310 Precision@10, 16 of 22, p_Holm = 0.011014), the hybrid is indistinguishable from it (−0.0038, 10 of 22, p = 0.750238) and collaborative filtering and both popularity baselines sit below it, so no cold-start conclusion in this thesis should be read as a statement that the hybrid ranks new learners well. <!-- C5.111, C5.112, C5.113 -->
   That comparison was not pre-registered, decides no criterion and revises no verdict. <!-- C5.116 -->
6. **The licence is verified for the dataset but not traced to this copy.** CC BY 4.0 was read on 2026-09-21 from the dataset authors' own deposit and corroborated by a mirror, but the bytes analysed were extracted from a local archive whose fetch URL is recorded nowhere and which contains no licence file, so the provenance chain has one unverified link. <!-- C5.104 -->
7. **RQ2 as `plan.md` words it cannot be answered from this design.** The plan has a behavioural segmentation supplying a cold-start prior to the hybrid and asks whether that segmentation improves the prior, but the protocol compares six models of which none is segmented, and chapter 3 section 3.1 states the consequence in its strongest form, that the behavioural module is descriptive only and **feeds no model**, no feature, cluster or segment label from it entering any compared model. This run therefore cannot answer RQ2, and it is reported as unanswerable rather than answered by proxy. <!-- C5.105 -->
8. **The conclusions are about one family of hybrid.** They hold for a linear score-level blend of cosine collaborative and cosine content-based scores with a validation-fitted weight, not for hybrids in general and for no neural architecture, since the deep models the proposal names were deferred by the candidate's recorded decision and none was run. <!-- C5.106 -->
9. **A fixed-alpha sweep was deliberately not run.** Asking whether some fixed blend weight would have beaten content-based filtering on cold start was judged boundable and test-safe but knob-turning dressed as diagnosis once C2 was closed, so it was excluded; it is an open question for the candidate and it is listed as future work rather than answered here. <!-- C5.107 -->
10. **The two diagnostics are post-hoc, on a different code tree, and decide nothing.** Section 5.8's disclosure is a limitation as much as a caveat: the D1 reading of why the hybrid sits between its components is a correlate, and the D2 result is about the blend's feature set and not about a behavioural recommender component. <!-- C5.108 -->
11. **Two elements of the execution chain are weaker than the design intends.** The workflow engine could not run on the machine used, so both receipts were written by hand from real file hashes, and the pre-run test, refusal-check and canary passes are unreceipted and therefore `unchecked`. <!-- C5.109 -->
12. **RQ1's descriptive half was only examined for three of the four behaviour families it names.** Module D computes click volume, active-day count and day-ordered sequence at the learner level — the last as two scalar summaries only, not an ordered-sequence model <!-- C5.132 --> — but activity type was never computed as a learner-level Module D feature; the only measured use of `activity_type` in this thesis is as a content-side input and as a diversity metric, neither of which describes learner behaviour, so nothing here says whether activity type would separate learners at all. <!-- C5.131 -->

This section was re-read against every result above after the diagnostics were added, and it contains no claim about scope that the tables of this chapter contradict.

## 5.12 What this chapter established

This chapter established, on two receipted runs of one reviewed code tree over all 22 OULAD module presentations, that the hybrid recommender is more accurate than both single-technique baselines on engaged warm-user slices — a median +31.20% relative over content-based filtering and +10.68% over collaborative filtering, both Holm-corrected significant, so criterion C1 is met as written — and that on the corrected cold-start condition it beats collaborative filtering by a median +7.32% while losing to content-based filtering by a median −30.28%, both at a Holm-corrected p of 0.000241, so criteria C2 and C3 are not met, C3 passing all four of its conjuncts in 2 of 22 presentations. <!-- C5.14, C5.15, C5.31, C5.39, C5.40, C5.41 -->
It established that this inverts the phase 1 pilot's reported pattern; that all six recorded phase 1 values were reproduced with an absolute difference of exactly 0.0 on EEE_2014J, the only presentation the reproduction covers, ruling out a difference in the pilot's arithmetic there rather than a code difference across all 22; and, from a new measured contrast (Table 5.14), that the median direction of the inversion tracks the evaluation split rather than the cold-start construction, with the corrected construction still changing the median gain against collaborative filtering from a median +7.32% under the refit condition to a median +13.34% under the pilot's construction — a difference of medians only, since the paired per-presentation shift has a median of about +0.01 percentage points (11 up, 11 down) and reaches about −53 percentage points in a single presentation (AAA_2014J) — but neither comparison's median direction on either split. <!-- C5.35, C5.36, C5.37 -->
It established that no leakage conjunct of C4 was violated, that the compute budget of C5 held with 1.0140 of 4.0 CPU-hours, and that the two post-hoc diagnostics decide nothing: the fitted blend weight does not converge near 1 on cold start but sits near an even mixture, and removing three learner-activity columns from the blend's feature set costs a median 0.001088 Precision@10 on warm users and nothing detectable on cold start. <!-- C5.42, C5.43, C5.75, C5.81, C5.82 -->
It established, exploratorily and deciding no criterion, that on the cold-start-refit slice only content-based filtering is clearly above a random floor, that the hybrid is indistinguishable from it, and that collaborative filtering and both popularity baselines sit below it, so the cold-start comparison the hybrid wins is a comparison with a baseline that does not clear chance and the popularity conjunct C3 clears is a weak floor. <!-- C5.111, C5.112, C5.113, C5.114, C5.115 -->

It did not establish why the hybrid loses to content-based filtering on new learners: D1 offers a reading and not a mechanism, the earlier label-collapse explanation is withdrawn on the evidence, and confirming any mechanism would require manipulating the blend weight on a closed criterion. It established, descriptively and deciding no criterion, that the six behavioural features separate the evaluated learners widely and yield a four-way segmentation in 22 of 22 presentations whose separation is weak to moderate (silhouette median 0.3274) and whose leading axis is activity volume, which answers the first half of RQ1 for three of the four families it names — click volume, active-day count and day-ordered sequence; activity type was not computed as a learner-level feature — and, because nothing from that module reaches a scored model and its features carry no time bound, leaves the second half unestablished. <!-- C5.123, C5.127, C5.129, C5.130 -->

It did not establish anything about the hypothesis's behavioural-signal clause, which stays `unchecked`; about learning, retention or attainment, which this design cannot measure; about disengaged learners or actual new registrations, which the filters and the cold-start construction exclude; about institutions other than the one the data came from; or about hybrids outside linear score-level blending. It could not answer RQ2 as the plan words it.

Chapter 6 takes from this chapter exactly these things and adds nothing to them: the six criterion verdicts of section 5.5 with their measured values, the central finding of section 5.4 with both baselines named, the generalisation evidence of section 5.6, the post-hoc status of section 5.8, and the twelve limitations of section 5.11, which bound every conclusion it draws.

## 3.0 What this chapter establishes

This chapter fixes how the recommenders of this thesis are compared, precisely enough that another
researcher could re-run the experiment and obtain the same numbers, and soundly enough that the
comparison answers the research questions of chapter 1. It states the research design and what it
cannot conclude (3.1), describes the dataset with its licence, its population and two defects in
its own documentation (3.2), maps every variable in the hypothesis to a column and records the one
that has no column (3.3), fixes the preprocessing and the deterministic choice of module
presentation (3.4), specifies the split rule and the two cold-start constructions (3.5), lists the
models compared and the hybrid's design (3.6), defines the metrics and states the ceiling on the
primary one (3.7), fixes the variance and significance plan before any run (3.8), states the
hardware and time budget (3.9) and closes with the ethics and data statement and the scope
reductions this thesis records (3.10 and 3.11). <!-- C3.1 -->

## 3.1 Research design

This is a **quantitative offline comparison of ranking models on logged learner–resource
interactions** in a virtual learning environment. The unit of analysis is a learner within one
module presentation. The replication unit is the 22 module presentations of the dataset. <!-- C3.2 --> The
population is UK Open University distance learners registered on those presentations in 2013 and
2014, narrowed by the filters of 3.4 to learners who interacted with at least five distinct
resources. <!-- C3.3 -->

The design answers the research questions of chapter 1 as follows: RQ0 and RQ3 by comparing the
hybrid against each single technique on the warm slice, RQ0 and RQ4 by the same comparison on the
cold-start slice with significance testing across the 22 presentations. <!-- C3.4 --> RQ1 is addressed
**descriptively only**: Module D describes the distributions of the behavioural features (click
volume, distinct active days and the day-ordered click sequence, the declared proxy of 3.3.1) per
module presentation and clusters learners on them for description. <!-- C3.5 --> **Module D feeds no model**: no
behavioural feature, cluster or segment label from it enters any of the compared models, and a
unit test enforces that its module is imported only by the evaluation code and reaches no model.
(The learner-activity inputs of the learned blend weight in the protocol's section 8.1, such as the
click total and the active-day count of the visible history, are computed inside the blend and are
not Module D outputs.) **RQ2, as `plan.md` words it** (whether segmenting learners by behavioural
profile improves the cold-start prior over a non-segmented hybrid), **is not answerable from this
design**, because no segmented model is compared and no cold-start prior is fitted from segments.
The research question itself is frozen in `plan.md` and is not reworded here; that RQ2 cannot be
answered is recorded as an open question for the human. <!-- C3.6 -->

Two things this design **cannot** conclude are stated here rather than left for the viva. First,
**offline ranking accuracy is not learning gain**: a click records that a resource was opened, not
that it was read, understood or useful, so no causal claim about learning, retention or attainment
follows from any number in this thesis. Second, the log was produced by the platform's own
navigation, so a resource a learner never opened may be an unobserved positive rather than a true
negative, and the label set is therefore incomplete by construction <!-- ledger: castells2022offline -->. <!-- C3.7 -->
Both limits are repeated in chapter 5 and in the threats table.

**On the status of C3.** C3 is an *operationalisation* of the second success criterion of the
frozen idea file, not a quotation of it. <!-- C3.16 --> The idea file requires that the hybrid "must produce
non-degenerate top-K recommendations (not empty, not identical for every user) and must not simply
fall back to global popularity ranking without at least matching the content-based-only baseline's
Precision@10 on that same cold-start slice" — one conditional clause with no numeric bar against
popularity and no stated aggregation. C3 turns that clause into four conjuncts a script can
evaluate. The operationalisation was necessary, because the clause could not be judged in phase 1
at all: no popularity baseline existed, and an unbuilt comparison is unchecked, never passed. It is
described as an operationalisation everywhere in this thesis, and **no threshold was moved** in
making it decidable. <!-- C3.17 --> `methodology/protocol.md` §10.5 records the reasoning in full.

**On the asymmetry between C3 and C1/C2.** C3 is judged in *each* of the 22 presentations, while
C1 and C2 are judged on a median over the 22 with a significance test. <!-- C3.18 --> This asymmetry is
deliberate and is recorded as named decision M-C3. C1 and C2 assert that the hybrid is *better by
a stated margin*, and estimating a margin is exactly what a median and a significance test are
for. <!-- C3.19 --> C3 asserts that the hybrid *never falls below* two reference points and never emits a
degenerate list — a floor and a structural guarantee, and a guarantee that holds only on median is
not a guarantee. <!-- C3.20 --> So that the three criteria can still be read against one another, C3's two
accuracy conjuncts are **additionally reported** in the C1/C2 style, as reporting that decides
nothing. Whether the supervisor prefers those two conjuncts to be *judged* that way instead is an
open question for the candidate (OQ6); relaxing them would change what C3 demands, which is a
decision reserved for the human.

## 3.2 Dataset

The experiments use the **Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD)**, published by
Kuzilek, Hlosta and Zdrahal in *Scientific Data* <!-- ledger: kuzilek2017oulad -->. <!-- C3.21 --> It records
learner demographics, daily click summaries of learner interactions with the virtual learning
environment, and assessment results for seven modules across 22 module presentations beginning in
February ("B") and October ("J") of 2013 and 2014. <!-- C3.22 -->

**Why OULAD.** The approved proposal names no dataset already in hand, and none of the nine
reviewed papers uses a shared, public e-learning dataset: they use adjacent-domain benchmarks or
private, institution-specific or web-mined data. OULAD is real, public, citable learner behaviour
data whose user-information and history-log tables map onto the variables the proposal names, so
the results of this thesis are a research finding rather than a pipeline demonstration. Chapter 2
found that work on OULAD which could be opened uses its behaviour data to predict learner
outcomes rather than to rank content <!-- ledger: qiu2022behaviour -->, which is part of the gap
this thesis addresses. <!-- C3.23 -->

**Licence.** OULAD is published by its authors under the **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
International (CC BY 4.0)** licence, verified on 2026-09-21 at the authors' own deposit (figshare,
DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.5081998.v1), whose licence field reads `"name": "CC BY 4.0"` with the URL
`https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/`, and corroborated by the UCI Machine Learning
Repository's page for the dataset. <!-- C3.24 --> The licence permits use, adaptation and redistribution,
including commercially, provided the authors are credited, the licence is linked and any changes
are indicated. One gap is recorded rather than hidden: the URL printed inside the distributed
archive now redirects to a page carrying no licence statement, the archive itself contains no
licence file, and the fetch URL of the local copy is not documented — so the licence is verified
for the dataset as deposited but not traced to this particular copy. This thesis redistributes no
part of the data. `methodology/data_statement.md` §2 holds the full quotations, URLs and dates.

**Population and its limitation.** The population is UK Open University distance learners. It is
**not** the candidate's institution, not on-campus learners and not a non-UK cohort. Any conclusion
of this thesis is a conclusion about distance learners on one platform in 2013 and 2014, and no
deployment decision elsewhere follows without re-validation on that institution's own data.

**Two defects in the dataset's own documentation.** Both were found by counting the tables rather
than reading the description, and both are stated here because an examiner who reads the schema
will find them.

1. **The student count.** `OULAD.names` states 32,953 students and 32,953 registration entries. <!-- C3.25 -->
   `studentInfo.csv` and `studentRegistration.csv` each hold **32,593** data rows, and
   `OULAD.names`'s own class-distribution table totals 32,593 — so the description contradicts both
   the data and itself, by 360 students. <!-- C3.26 -->
   Four other figures in the same section of the description are correct: 6,364 VLE items,
   10,655,280 log entries, 206 assessments, 173,912 assessment entries. <!-- C3.27 --> The journal descriptor cited in chapter 2 also gives 32,593, so it is the archive's description
   file alone that disagrees. <!-- C3.28 --> **The figure used
   throughout this thesis is 32,593**, and every count in this chapter names the file it came from. <!-- C3.29 -->
2. **The granularity of the interaction log.** The description implies one row per learner,
   resource and day. The file does not honour that: in the presentation used for the single-slice
   reproduction, 402,947 rows reduce to 303,660 distinct (learner, resource, day) triples, of which
   74,972 carry more than one row, up to ten; across the whole file, 1,614,505 of 8,459,320 triples
   are duplicated. <!-- C3.30 --> Every click total in this
   thesis is therefore an explicit sum over a group, and every active-day count is a count of
   **distinct** days, never a row count.

**Provenance record.** The eight tables are hashed in `inputs/manifest.json`; all eight SHA256
values were recomputed from the files on 2026-09-21 and match. <!-- C3.31 --> `methodology/protocol.md` §2.1 lists
each file with its row count, byte size, hash and role, and marks `assessments.csv` and
`studentAssessment.csv` as not used.

## 3.3 Variables

The proposal's variables map onto columns as follows. *User history logs* become `sum_click` summed
per learner and resource in `studentVle.csv`; *time and context* become that table's `date`, an
integer day offset from the presentation's start which may be negative and is never clipped — in
one presentation alone 19,986 of 402,947 rows carry a negative day; *item content* becomes `activity_type`,
`week_from` and `week_to` in `vle.csv`, where `activity_type` takes 20 values; and
*module length*, used only to bound a derived course week, comes from `courses.csv`. <!-- C3.32 -->

**User information is deliberately not used as a model feature.** No column of `studentInfo.csv` —
`gender`, `region`, `highest_education`, `imd_band`, `age_band`, `num_of_prev_attempts`,
`studied_credits`, `disability` or `final_result` — enters any model as a feature, a filter or a
stratifier. Three reasons: the approved plan's modules specify no demographic feature, so adding
one would be scope drift; whether protected and proxy-protected attributes may be used at all is an
ethics decision reserved for the candidate and not yet answered, and an unanswered question is not
permission; and `final_result` is an outcome variable whose use as an input would leak exactly what
an educational recommender is ultimately judged on. `studentInfo.csv` is read only to count how
many learners exist. If the candidate later authorises demographic features, this methodology is
revised and a fairness check reported per group becomes mandatory, whatever it shows.

### 3.3.1 Recorded scope reduction: the behavioural signal

The hypothesis names a behavioural session signal of "browsing sequence, dwell time, engagement
pattern". Two of those three exist in the data as proxies and one does not exist at all.

| Signal named in the hypothesis | Recorded in OULAD? | What is measured instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browsing sequence | indirectly | the learner's resources ordered by the day of first interaction, ties broken by resource id |
| Engagement pattern | indirectly | summed clicks per learner and resource, and the learner's count of distinct active days |
| **Dwell time** | **no** | summed clicks; the dataset holds no duration field anywhere |

`studentVle.csv` carries exactly six columns — `code_module`, `code_presentation`, `id_student`,
`id_site`, `date`, `sum_click` — and no notion of time on page. Following the candidate's recorded
decision, **click volume, active days and click sequence are the behavioural proxy, the
substitution is recorded here as a scope reduction, and the hypothesis stays frozen**. <!-- C3.33 --> The cost is
stated plainly: a count cannot distinguish a resource opened once by accident from one studied for
an hour, so the behavioural signal in this thesis is thinner than the hypothesis describes. The
phrase "dwell time" is never used in this thesis for anything that was measured. <!-- C3.34 -->

## 3.4 Preprocessing and the choice of module presentation

The modelling record is the **(learner, resource) pair**, not the raw log row. Each pair's ordering
key is the earliest day on which the learner touched that resource, and its click weight is the sum
of `sum_click` over its rows **inside a stated bound**. The bound is part of the definition
and is never omitted: for every training use the bound is the learner's first validation day and a
row is inside it when its date is strictly before that day; for the visible history of a cold-start
learner the bound is the day of their k-th visible interaction and a row is inside it when its date
is on or before that day. <!-- C3.35 -->
Each learner's active-day count is the number of distinct days inside the same bound.
Four runs have no per-learner validation day (the secondary global cut-off split, the tertiary random split, the two-day sensitivity run and the phase 1 reproduction); their bounds are the global validation cut-off day, none, the learner's second day and none respectively, as protocol section 5.5 states.

**Why the bound matters.** An earlier draft summed clicks over *all* of a pair's rows while the
pair's first day alone decided which slice it belonged to, so a resource entered training by its
first visit but carried the weight of every later revisit. Measured on EEE_2014J, 17.8% of the
clicks on warm training pairs are dated on or after that learner's test boundary, and at k = 2,
96.9% of the clicks carried by the two visible resources are dated after the second interaction. <!-- C3.36 --> A learner described as having two prior
interactions was therefore weighted by their later engagement. The leak was invisible to the
ordering check, which inspects first days and not the rows behind them, and it was **asymmetric
across the compared models**: the popularity baseline counts distinct learners and reads no click,
so only the content-based, collaborative and hybrid models carried it — which bears directly on the
comparisons that decide criteria C2 and C3. A counted assertion now reports, for every fitted and
scored quantity, the number of rows read from beyond the bound, which must be zero. <!-- C3.37 -->

Preprocessing applies six rules, each with a reason and a counted effect:

1. **Presentation selection for the reproduction** is deterministic and pre-registered: the
   presentation whose interaction count is closest to 400,000, ties broken by name. <!-- C3.38 --> This selects
   **EEE_2014J** with 402,947 interactions, the nearest competitor being BBB_2013B at 403,266. <!-- C3.39 --> The rule is fixed before any result, which
   is what makes it a defence against selecting a presentation on its outcome; the main experiment
   runs on all 22 presentations regardless, so the choice affects only the single-slice
   reproduction of criterion C6. <!-- C3.40 -->
2. **Catalogue restriction** keeps interactions whose resource appears in that presentation's own
   catalogue. This removes zero rows in all 22 presentations, verified. <!-- C3.41 -->
3. **Aggregation** collapses rows to pairs as described above: in EEE_2014J, 402,947 rows become
   47,136 pairs over 1,097 learners. <!-- C3.42 -->
4. **Learner filter, in two pre-registered parts.** <!-- C3.43 --> The first keeps learners with at least **five
   distinct resources**; in EEE_2014J it removes 48 of the 1,097 learners who appear in the log,
   and across the 22 presentations it removes 1,019 of 29,228. <!-- C3.44 --> The second keeps learners with at
   least **three distinct first-interaction days**, which is what the day-based split of 3.5.1
   needs to produce a non-empty training, validation and test set for that learner; in EEE_2014J it
   removes a further 31, leaving **1,018**, and across the 22 presentations it removes **997
   (3.53%), leaving 27,212 evaluated learners**. <!-- C3.45 --> Stating the day requirement as a
   filter rather than letting the split drop learners silently is what keeps the evaluated
   population well defined; the split's own exclusion count is then zero and is asserted to be so. <!-- C3.46 -->
   The first threshold is varied to 3 and to 10 as a sensitivity check. <!-- C3.47 -->
   **The second exclusion is not random and must be reported as a limitation.** The 997 excluded
   learners hold a median of 8 distinct resources spread over a median of **two** active days, none
   over more than 14 days, against 55 resources over 52 days for the 27,212 retained. <!-- C3.48 --> They are learners who opened a handful of
   resources in one or two sittings and did not come back — close to the disengaged population a
   recommender is meant to reach. <!-- C3.49 --> No result in this thesis speaks for them.
5. **Missing week values.** `?` in `week_from` or `week_to` becomes missing, then the median of the
   known values in that presentation; when a column has **no** known value the fallback is `0.0`,
   and a binary "week missing" indicator is always a feature. <!-- C3.50 --> This is the fix for a defect found in
   phase 1: the median of an all-missing column is itself undefined, propagated through every item
   vector and every norm, and made the hybrid bit-for-bit identical to content-based filtering in
   three presentations. Those three are named: `week_from` and `week_to` are unknown for **100.0%**
   of resources in BBB_2013B, DDD_2013B and DDD_2013J, and almost so in DDD_2014B (0.2% known) and
   DDD_2014J (0.3% known). <!-- C3.51 --> The code must assert that
   no feature matrix contains an undefined value, rather than repairing one silently. <!-- C3.52 -->
6. **No resource filter.** Every catalogue item stays a candidate even with no training
   interactions, because removing such items would hide exactly the case content-based filtering
   exists to solve.

## 3.5 Split rule and cold-start slices

### 3.5.1 The split

The primary split is a **per-learner day-boundary split**, applied independently within each
presentation. **Whole days are assigned, never divided.** Each learner's (learner, resource) pairs
are grouped by the day of first interaction; a test cut is chosen at the day boundary whose
resulting test set is closest to 20% of that learner's pairs, and a validation cut at the boundary
whose validation set is closest to 10%, with everything earlier forming the training set. <!-- C3.77 --> The test
cut may fall no earlier than the learner's **third** distinct day, which is what guarantees that a
validation day remains between the training and test blocks. Because whole days are assigned, a
strict guarantee follows by construction: **for every learner, the latest training or validation
day is strictly earlier than the earliest test day.** Day granularity
is coarse, so the realised proportions are close to but not exactly the targets; they are therefore
reported per presentation rather than assumed. Across the 22, the realised test fraction runs from
**0.199 to 0.205** and the realised validation fraction from **0.102 to 0.120**. <!-- C3.78 --> **The split requires no random seed.**

Assigning whole days is what makes the guarantee provable, but it needs three distinct days per
learner, which is why that requirement is a stated population filter (3.4, item 4) applied before
the split rather than a silent loss inside it. With the third-day bound on the test cut, three
distinct days is **sufficient** as well as necessary, so the filter and the split describe exactly
the same learners: verified over all 27,212, no learner has an empty training, validation or test
set and none violates the ordering guarantee. <!-- C3.79 --> The split's own exclusion count is consequently zero,
and the code asserts it. <!-- C3.80 -->

This replaces the phase 1 design, which held out a random 20% of each learner's resources and
ignored the day column entirely — so a model could be trained on resources the learner
opened *after* the ones it had to predict. <!-- C3.81 --> That is temporal leakage, and the evaluation literature
shows it changes not only the absolute numbers but the relative ordering of models
<!-- ledger: ji2023leakage -->. <!-- C3.82 --> Two further splits are reported: a **global cut-off** at the day
below which 80% of the presentation's interaction rows fall, the closest analogue of deployment, as
a robustness check; and the phase 1 **random holdout**, kept only so the earlier numbers remain
comparable and so the cost of that leakage can be measured. <!-- C3.83 --> Every table that shows the random split
labels it temporally leaky, and **no criterion is judged on it**. <!-- C3.84 -->

**Which split governs, declared in advance.** <!-- C3.85 --> Criteria C1 to C3 are judged on the **primary** split
alone, which is what the plan's own `on_dataset` fields name. <!-- C3.86 --> If the global cut-off split reverses
the sign of a comparison that decides a criterion, both results are reported side by side in
chapter 5, the criterion verdict still stands on the primary split, and the reversal is reported as
a limitation on the robustness of that finding. Fixing this rule before the run is what prevents a
later choice of whichever split gives the preferred answer.

**A residual the primary split does not remove.** The primary split is leakage-free **within** a
learner, not across the cohort. Every learner shares the presentation's calendar, so the item–item
similarity, the popularity counts and the blend model are fitted on *other* learners' training and
validation pairs, some of which postdate a given learner's test items. The global cut-off split is
the one that removes this, which is exactly why it is run. Where this chapter and chapter 5 say the
splits are strictly time-ordered, that is true **per learner**; it is not a claim about the cohort,
and the difference is recorded as threat T1.

**Identical splits.** The frozen idea file requires that all compared models be trained and
evaluated on the same split. <!-- C3.87 --> This methodology honours that in its strongest form: the split is
built once per presentation and every model — random, global popularity, week-aware popularity,
content-based, collaborative and hybrid — receives byte-identical training, validation and test
sets, proved by comparing the hash of each serialised set across models before scoring. Nine
leakage assertions (L1 to L9) are executed, and each writes a *measured count* to the run log,
including when that count is zero: strict temporal ordering per learner; every fitted object reading
only its declared source rows; every preprocessing constant reading only its declared source;
pairwise disjoint splits; identical split hashes across models; the test set scored exactly once per
model; in the cold-start refit condition, no fitted object reading a held-out learner's row; the
candidate exclusion set equal to the information set of each of the three prediction moments; and
no click weight or active-day count reading a row dated after its bound. To make
the second and third of these countable rather than merely asserted, every function that fits a
model or computes a constant takes its permitted row set as an argument and returns the number of
rows it read from outside that set, which must be zero. <!-- C3.88 -->

### 3.5.2 The cold-start slices

Cold start is the condition in which a learner has very little interaction history, and the reviewed
literature identifies it as the main failure mode of single-technique recommenders
<!-- ledger: panda2022coldstart -->. <!-- C3.89 --> This thesis reports **two constructions**, which support
different claims, and says which is which everywhere.

**Condition A — held-out new learners with the model refitted (primary; decides C2 and C3).** <!-- C3.90 -->
Within each presentation, 20% of the evaluated learners are set aside as cold-start learners by a
seeded draw stratified by their training-activity quintile, so the cold cohort matches the warm
cohort's activity distribution; cohort sizes run from 69 (AAA_2014J) to 426 (CCC_2014J). <!-- C3.91 --> **Every fitted quantity is then rebuilt from
the remaining 80% of learners only** — the item–item similarity model, the global and week-aware
popularity counts, the selection of the collaborative neighbourhood size and of the blend model's
regulariser, and the blend model itself with its feature scaler. <!-- C3.92 --> The cold-start learners' rows are
removed from each of those, not merely hidden at scoring time, and each removal is a separate
assertion emitting a count that must be zero; `methodology/protocol.md` §6.1a lists the eight
quantities, the learner set each is fitted on and its assertion. <!-- C3.93 --> Two of the eight — the item
feature vectors' week medians and normalisation bounds, and the per-learner score scaling inside
the blend — are derived from the resource catalogue or from the learner's own scores and depend on
no other learner at all; they are stated as such, with the count that proves it, rather than left
ambiguous. Each cold-start
learner is then shown the **earliest k resources by day**, never a random k, and is scored on their
own time-based test set, which is held fixed across k so the curve is a paired comparison. The
curve runs over k of 0, 1, 2 and 5; **k = 2 decides C2 and C3**, matching the idea file's "at most
two prior interactions". <!-- C3.94 --> A robustness variant selects the cohort as the latest-arriving 20% of
learners instead, which is the more literal picture of a new registration. <!-- C3.95 -->

**Condition B — simulated thin history (secondary; labelled optimistic).** The phase 1
construction, kept for continuity: the same learners as the warm slice, the same test sets, and one
similarity model fitted on **every** evaluated learner's full training history, with the visible
history truncated only at scoring time. No held-out interaction enters that model, so this
construction passes every leakage check — and it still cannot support a claim about new learners,
because the learners it calls new shaped the model that ranks for them. Every number from Condition
B is labelled "simulated thin history (optimistic)" and decides nothing.

Because both conditions run on identical splits with identical test sets and identical k, the
difference between them **is** the optimism of the cheap construction, measured rather than
asserted; chapter 5 reports it at k = 2, for the hybrid, CBF and ItemKNN-CF, per presentation, on
the primary and tertiary splits (Table 5.14).
Table 3.2 states what each condition was fitted on, which is the distinction that makes them
readable.

*Table 3.2 — What each condition was fitted on, and what it can claim.*

| Condition | Learners in **every** fitted quantity | Visible history at scoring | Test set | Supports a claim about |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | all evaluated learners | all training pairs | time-based test set | an established learner |
| A (primary) | the 80% warm learners only, for all five learner-dependent fits | earliest k by day | same test set, fixed across k | a genuinely new learner <!-- C3.96 --> |
| A robustness | the 80% earliest-arriving learners, same five fits | earliest k by day | same test set | a new learner arriving late in the presentation <!-- C3.97 --> |
| B (optimistic) | **all** learners, including the cold ones | earliest k by day | same test set | thin history only — **not** a new learner |

### 3.5.3 The task and the candidate set

For each evaluated learner, every catalogue resource the learner's visible history does not already
contain is ranked, and the measure is how many of the learner's held-out later resources appear in
the top K. **The full catalogue is ranked — 106 to 529 resources depending on the presentation —
and no negatives are sampled**, because full ranking is computationally free at this size and
sampling can reorder models, which would make every comparison contingent on the sample. <!-- C3.98 --> Resources
already seen are excluded from the candidate list, so the task is *recommend an unseen resource*
rather than *predict the next click*; these are different tasks with different ceilings, and this
thesis performs the first. Because the split is defined on (learner, resource) pairs, a resource can
be in a learner's training set or their test set but never both, so the exclusion never removes a
ground-truth item.

**What "already seen" covers, and why it differs between the warm and cold conditions.** One
principle governs the exclusion set: **a learner's candidates exclude exactly the resources that
learner has already opened as of the moment the recommendation is made**, and nothing else. A
recommender at that moment knows the learner's past and nothing of their future, so everything
excluded must lie in the past and nothing in the future may be used — not to rank, and not to
filter.

The principle yields **three** moments, because a score is computed either against validation
ground truth — when the blend weight, the neighbourhood size or the regulariser is being chosen —
or against test ground truth, and the learner's information state differs between them. At the
**warm validation** moment the learner has opened their training resources only, so only those are
excluded; excluding the validation resources here would exclude the ground truth itself. At the
**warm test** moment the learner has by then opened both their training and their validation
resources, and both are excluded, while the models are still fitted on training resources alone,
which is the conservative direction. In the **cold-start** conditions the moment is immediately
after the learner's k-th interaction at *either* boundary, the learner has supplied exactly k
resources, and **only those k are excluded** — their later training resources and their validation
resources lie in that learner's future and are invisible to a genuinely new learner.

**This corrects a defect in the previous draft, and the correction is large.** An earlier draft
applied the warm rule in every condition, reasoning that a filter applied to all six models could
not bias a comparison. That reasoning fails for a new learner: it struck roughly thirty of their
later resources from every model's candidate list, so the models were ranking after an oracle had
already removed most of the wrong answers, and a learner whose candidate set encodes thirty later
interactions is not the "new user with at most two prior interactions" the frozen criterion names. <!-- C3.99 -->
Measured on EEE_2014J at k = 2, cold-start Precision@10 under the two rules was 0.0255 against
0.2157 for the popularity baseline and 0.0333 against 0.1946 for collaborative filtering — an
inflation of between 5.8 and 8.5 times. <!-- C3.100 --> Worse, the inflated rule converged those two
models to within 11% of each other, where the corrected rule leaves collaborative filtering 31%
above popularity on its own merits; criterion C3 would have lost much of its power to test the
hybrid against popularity. <!-- C3.101 --> A second, opposite error had then to be corrected: applying the warm
test-boundary rule at the *validation* boundary put the ground truth inside its own exclusion set
and drove validation Precision@10 to exactly zero, which would have made the warm blend weight,
neighbourhood size and regulariser degenerate without any check failing. Since C1, C2 and C3 all
rest on these scores, the per-moment rule is load-bearing. It is recorded as threat T27.

Two consequences are stated rather than hidden. Honest cold-start Precision@10 values are small —
of the order of 0.025 to 0.033 on EEE_2014J, with other presentations and models differing, as chapter 5 reports — because the target is the set of resources a learner first
opens *after* the boundary, so resources opened in the middle of their history count as misses for
every model equally; this is a property of the task, not a failure of the models, and chapter 5
says so before quoting any cold-start number. <!-- C3.102 --> And in the warm condition the candidate set differs
between the validation and test moments by the learner's validation resources, so the blend model's
score-mass features shift slightly between fitting and scoring; that shift is a correct consequence
of measuring each score as of its own moment, it is reported rather than removed, and it is
recorded as threat T29.

## 3.6 Models compared and the hybrid's design

Six models are compared on identical splits. Four are the models the approved plan names; the
random floor and the week-aware popularity variant are additions for reporting and **neither
decides any criterion**. The criterion C3's "global-popularity baseline" is the global popularity
model and only that.

| Model | Specification | Tuned on validation | Deterministic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | uniform draw over the candidate set, five seeds, mean and standard deviation reported | nothing | no |
| Global popularity | rank by the number of **distinct training learners** who used the resource, not by total clicks, so one heavy user cannot manufacture popularity | nothing | yes |
| Week-aware popularity | popularity restricted to resources whose week window contains the learner's current course week, with unknown-week resources always eligible | nothing | yes |
| Content-based | cosine between a click-weighted learner profile and item vectors built from one-hot `activity_type`, normalised `week_from` and `week_to`, and a week-missing indicator | nothing | yes |
| Collaborative (item-based) | cosine similarity between resource columns of the learner–resource click matrix, scores summed over visible resources with a truncated neighbourhood <!-- ledger: sarwar2001itembased --> | neighbourhood size, from five values | yes <!-- C3.103 --> |
| **Hybrid** | score-level blend of the min–max scaled collaborative and content scores <!-- ledger: burke2002hybrid --> | the blend weight and its regulariser | yes <!-- C3.104 --> |

**The hybrid, and the object that produces its blend weight.** The hybrid blends the two component
scores linearly after min–max scaling, with a weight α that is a **single scalar per learner**, not
a per-resource quantity. α is produced by a logistic regression fitted on the **validation split
only**, and the full specification — target, features, scaling, estimator settings, selection
procedure and degenerate cases — is in `methodology/protocol.md` §8.1, because a blend that cannot
be rebuilt identically by another implementer is a blend that can be tuned after the fact. In
outline: for each validation learner, a fixed eleven-point grid of candidate α values is swept
against that learner's **validation** resources, and the best value is recorded and binarised into
a label meaning "this learner is better served by a collaborative-leaning blend"; a logistic
regression over eight learner-level features — visible-resource count, log visible click total,
active-day count, each component's top-1 and top-10 score mass, and the share of the catalogue with
missing week data — is fitted to that label with z-scored features whose means and deviations come
from the validation rows alone; and **α is then the model's predicted probability**, which lies in
[0,1] by construction and needs no clipping. <!-- C3.105 --> Its regulariser is chosen by stratified five-fold
cross-validation *within* the validation learners, so no selection touches the test split; the
hybrid's collaborative component **inherits** the neighbourhood size already selected for the
standalone collaborative baseline rather than searching its own; and the feature definition is
identical at fitting and at scoring time within a condition, only the ground truth differing. <!-- C3.106 -->
Learners whose validation Precision@10 is zero at every candidate α are **dropped from the fit**
rather than labelled, because the median of an all-tied grid would otherwise label them
"collaborative-leaning" purely because no blend could serve them; the share dropped is reported per
presentation, condition and k, and those learners are still scored and still counted in every
metric. There is **one α model, and one neighbourhood size, per presentation, condition and k**: in
each cold-start condition both are refitted with visible history truncated to that k, so neither is
applied outside the range it was fitted in — a hybrid that lost at cold start because its blend was
extrapolating would be evidence about specification rather than about the architecture.

**Ranking, ties and degenerate scores.** Three rules that decide a Precision@10 are fixed here
rather than left to the implementation. <!-- C3.107 --> Candidates are ordered by **score descending, then resource
id ascending** — a total order, identical for all six models and independent of any model's score,
so no model is favoured, applied by every model including popularity. This matters because at k of
0, 1 and 2 ties spanning the tenth place are the normal case rather than the exception. Min–max
scaling of a score vector whose range is zero returns the **zero vector**; consequently at k = 0
the collaborative, content-based and hybrid models all produce a constant score and therefore the
same fixed, resource-id-ordered list, which is the correct behaviour for a personalised model given
no history and is exactly why k = 0 is the honest place to read what popularity alone achieves. The
random baseline is scored by a seeded draw per candidate rather than shuffled, so it never ties and
is reproducible from its seed. The tie-break is arbitrary but pre-registered, and because it is
identical across models it moves absolute cold-start values without moving comparisons; this is
recorded as threat T28. <!-- C3.108 -->

This replaces the phase 1 weight, a hand-written formula of the visible-item count that was chosen
*after* the phase 1 test numbers had been seen. Anything fitted after test scores are known is
exploratory: the phase 1 weight is reported in chapter 4 labelled as such and supports no criterion
in this thesis, and the phase 2 weight never sees the test set. Whether the residual gap the pilot
found is a real ceiling of linear blending or an imperfectly guessed constant is precisely RQ3, and
this design is what makes the question answerable.

**Tuning parity, counted.** A *configuration* means one distinct hyperparameter setting evaluated
on validation, counted once however many cross-validation folds evaluate it. On that definition the
collaborative model uses five (its grid of neighbourhood sizes), the hybrid four (its grid of
regularisers, inheriting the neighbourhood size), and the content-based, popularity and random
models none — so the stated ceiling of five configurations per model per presentation holds, and
the hybrid has the smaller of the two tuned budgets. Disclosed separately rather than folded in:
the eleven-point α grid above is a **label-construction** sweep, not a hyperparameter search; it
selects no setting of any model, runs on validation resources only, and its output is a regression
target. Both counts are written to the run log per model per presentation, so the parity claim is
auditable rather than asserted — the failure mode a reproducibility analysis of the field found
across twelve neural recommenders, eleven of which were outperformed by well-tuned simple methods
<!-- ledger: ferrari2021troubling -->. <!-- C3.109 --> No selected configuration is refitted on training plus
validation, and this policy is identical for every model, which leaves the blend weight as the only
object in the design that has seen validation data — exactly what criterion C4 audits.

**Deferred scope.** The user interface promised in the proposal and the deep models it names —
recurrent, convolutional and radial-basis-function networks — are **deferred scope-outs** by the
candidate's recorded decision. They are named here with their reason: the plan's compute bound is a
single CPU with no GPU and no downloaded weights, and the plan reserves deep learning for a later
phase. They are listed as future work in chapter 6. The models compared in this thesis are the six
above, four of which are the plan's.

## 3.7 Metrics

**Precision@10** (Arabic: الضبط عند 10) is the primary metric, fixed by the approved plan, and it
decides C1, C2 and C3. <!-- C3.110 --> Following the terminology fixed in chapter 2, *precision* is rendered
**الضبط** and is not the same quantity as *accuracy* (**الدقة**); chapter 2 records two published
examples where the two diverge sharply in a single reported run. For a learner, Precision@10 is the
number of held-out resources appearing in the top ten, divided by ten.

Eight further measures are reported, at K of 5, 10 and 20 where a K applies: **Recall@K**, the share
of a learner's held-out resources that were retrieved; **NDCG@K** with binary relevance, which is
rank-sensitive and whose ideal discounted gain accounts for learners with fewer than K held-out
resources; **HitRate@K**, whether the model helped at all; **MRR@K**, the reciprocal rank of the
first hit, which is sensitive to the very top of the list a learner actually sees; **catalogue
coverage** and **intra-list diversity** over `activity_type`, which together provide the evidence
for C3's non-degeneracy conjuncts; the **empty-list count**; and a **popularity-agreement
diagnostic** — the rank correlation and top-ten overlap between the hybrid's cold-start ranking and
the popularity ranking — which measures the idea file's literal "must not simply fall back to global
popularity" clause directly. <!-- C3.111 --> Chapter 2 records that the closest published e-learning hybrid reported
only P@5 and that session-based work reports precision, recall and MRR at 5, 10 and 20
<!-- ledger: sanguino2022course --> <!-- ledger: kumar2023session -->, so this metric set is at or
above the reporting standard of the comparable literature. <!-- C3.112 -->

**Adding metrics is reporting, not threshold-moving.** C1, C2 and C3 remain judged on Precision@10
exactly as the plan states. The additional metrics exist so that a conclusion is not an artefact of
one ceiling-bound measure, and none of them may be used to re-decide a criterion after the fact.

Every metric is computed per learner and then averaged over learners; the median and the full
distribution accompany every mean, and per-presentation values are always published in full. This
is the lesson of a phase 1 defect in which one bug moved the mean relative gain against
collaborative filtering to −14.8% while the median stayed near −2.9%. <!-- C3.113 -->

### 3.7.1 The ceiling on the primary metric

A learner with fewer than ten held-out resources **cannot** reach a Precision@10 of 1; their
maximum is their held-out count divided by ten. This is not a marginal case. Computed on the
day-boundary split of 3.5.1 over the evaluated population of 3.4, in EEE_2014J **45.6%** of
evaluated learners have fewer than ten held-out resources, and across the 22 presentations the
share runs from **14.4%** (FFF_2013B) to **81.3%** (GGG_2014B). <!-- C3.114 --> Between 0.3% and 4.3% of learners, depending
on the presentation, have exactly one held-out resource, capping their Precision@10 at 0.1. <!-- C3.115 --> The
per-presentation table is in `methodology/protocol.md` §9.1.

Three consequences follow, and all three are honoured. Recall@K and NDCG@K are reported alongside,
because they normalise on what was available. The distribution of held-out-set sizes is published
next to every Precision table. And a Precision@10 difference **between presentations** is never
read as a difference in model quality, because it is partly a difference in how much of the metric
was reachable. <!-- C3.116 --> Precision@10 stays primary because the plan fixed it; this section is the caveat
that makes it readable, not a replacement for it.

## 3.8 Variance and significance plan

The plan is fixed here, before any run, so that no test is selected after its result is known. <!-- C3.117 -->

**Families of comparisons are declared in advance**, and the Holm correction is applied within each
family and nowhere else: family F1 is the two warm comparisons that decide C1; family F2 the two
cold-start comparisons that decide C2; family F3 the six within-presentation per-learner
comparisons reported for context; and family F4 everything else — other K values, other metrics, the
k curve, the secondary and tertiary splits and the optimistic condition — which is labelled
exploratory and supports no criterion. <!-- C3.118 -->

**The deciding test for C1 and C2** is a two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the 22
presentation-level values, Holm-corrected within its family. <!-- C3.119 --> The approved plan states that the
criterion's median is taken over the 22 presentations but does not name the test's unit; the
presentation is chosen because using a different unit for the median and for the test would make
the criterion internally inconsistent, and because with 22 paired observations it is the **less
powerful** of the two available readings, so the choice cannot be characterised as loosening the
bar. <!-- C3.120 --> An exact binomial sign test over the same 22 signed differences accompanies it as a
distribution-free companion. <!-- C3.121 -->

**What the test is run on, and how ties are handled.** The deciding Wilcoxon runs on the **paired
absolute** presentation-level Precision@10 values, not on the relative gains: the criterion's
*threshold* is a statement about the size of a relative gain, while its *test* asks whether the
paired difference is distinguishable from zero, and running the test on ratios would also make it
undefined wherever a baseline scores zero. Zero differences are not hypothetical here — phase 1
produced hybrid and content-based values that were bit-identical in the three presentations whose
week fields are entirely missing — so the rules are fixed in advance. <!-- C3.122 --> Zero differences are
**discarded** by both the Wilcoxon and the sign test, and **the effective number of pairs after
discarding is reported beside every p-value**, because it can fall below 22 and it changes the
smallest attainable p. <!-- C3.123 --> Below an effective six pairs the two-sided exact test cannot reach p < 0.05
at any effect size; such a result is reported as *inconclusive* with its effective n, and the
criterion's test conjunct is recorded as **unchecked** rather than as a failure, since an
unperformable check is not a negative result. <!-- C3.124 --> Where a baseline's Precision@10 is zero the relative
gain is undefined; that presentation is excluded from the median, the number excluded is reported
beside it, and no value is imputed or capped.

**Reported alongside, deciding nothing:** within each presentation, a two-sided paired Wilcoxon
signed-rank test on per-learner Precision@10 with Holm correction inside family F3, reported with a
rank-biserial effect size and the median per-learner difference; and a bootstrap 95% percentile
confidence interval over learners, 10,000 resamples, for every reported number, so that no value
appears in this thesis as a bare point estimate. <!-- C3.125 -->

**Why an effect size accompanies every p-value.** The per-learner tests run at between 347 and
2,131 paired learners. <!-- C3.126 --> Significance tests of this family are known to show inflated Type-I
error at large sample sizes, with effect size the appropriate judge of practical significance
<!-- ledger: ihemelandu2023inference -->. <!-- C3.127 --> The phase 1 median gap against collaborative filtering
was −2.7%; a gap of that size across a thousand paired learners can be statistically detectable and
practically negligible at the same time, and chapter 5 reports both. <!-- C3.128 -->

**Stochastic models** run five seeds with the mean and standard deviation across seeds, and every
seed appears in the results, never the best one. Only the random baseline is stochastic; the other
five models are deterministic and say so. <!-- C3.129 -->

**A limit on the replication unit.** The 22 presentations are not independent: they are 22
presentations of only seven modules, and two presentations of the same module share a catalogue and
a syllabus. <!-- C3.130 --> The across-22 tests are therefore reported as *indicative of generalisation*, not as a
random sample of courses, and a breakdown over the seven modules is published beside the 22-row
table so a reader can see whether a result is driven by one module. <!-- C3.131 -->

**Post-hoc additions, outside the pre-declared plan.** Three things were added after the main run and
were not declared before it. <!-- C3.132 --> All belong to the exploratory family F4, decide no criterion, and may not
revise any criterion, threshold, model or this protocol (integrity rules 3 and 8). <!-- C3.133 --> The first two are
diagnostics that the candidate approved after the main run, run as a separate stage on the approved
entry point. <!-- C3.134 --> D1 logs the blend weight that the production hybrid actually used for every learner scored
on the cold-start refit cell at k = 2. <!-- C3.135 --> D2 refits the blend without the three columns that carry the
behavioural proxy (the visible-resource count, the log of the visible clicks and the active-day count)
and scores it beside the production hybrid on the warm cell and on the k = 2 cold-start cell. <!-- C3.136 --> Both
score the test split a second time, outside the counter that enforces the rule of one scoring per
model, so they are second looks at the test split; the C4 verdict of the main run is unaffected
because the diagnostics run in their own process with no counter. <!-- C3.137 --> D2 removes columns from the blend's
own feature set and does not remove a behavioural recommender component, so it does not test the
behavioural clause of the hypothesis on its terms, which stays unchecked. <!-- C3.138 --> The third addition is a
comparison of every compared model with the random ranker on the warm cell and on the k = 2 cold-start
cell. <!-- C3.139 --> It re-reads the main run's already scored results, makes no new run and no refit, was written
after the results were seen, reports uncorrected p-values, and serves only to say how much a
criterion's passing conjunct is worth. Chapter 4 (section 4.7) gives the design and the disclosure of
the diagnostics, and chapter 5 gives their results.

## 3.9 Hardware, reproducibility and time budget

Every number in this thesis is produced on a **single CPU with no GPU**, no downloaded model
weights and no network access at run time; the dataset tables are already on disk and hashed. The
environment is Python 3.12.3 with NumPy 2.5.3, pandas 3.0.5, scikit-learn 1.9.1 and SciPy 1.18.1,
verified in the interpreter used for the runs on 2026-09-21. <!-- C3.140 -->

preprocessing entry point; the day-boundary split rule, which needs no seed; the seed list for the
three objects that need one; the code version; the library versions; the hardware; the time budget;
and the exact command line behind every reported number, recorded in a run receipt with the hash of
every artifact it produced. <!-- C3.141 -->

hash: it covers the first 10,257,030 bytes, the length of the file at the end of the main run. <!-- C3.146 --> The

The time budget is criterion C5: at most **4.0 CPU-hours** for the main run and at most **15

time summed over the run's process tree, divided by 3,600 — and not wall-clock, a distinction that
matters because the machine has twelve logical cores and a parallel step can consume several
core-minutes per wall-clock minute. <!-- C3.150 --> Both figures are recorded in every receipt, C5 is judged on

file's own constraint is expressed that way. <!-- C3.151 --> The budget must cover the main run over all 22

answers whether the filter choice moves the conclusion. <!-- C3.152 --> Phase 1 took about 29 seconds for one
presentation and 140 seconds for all 22 in a materially simpler design, so the ceiling is headroom
rather than a guess — but headroom is not a licence to overrun, so the experiment runner projects
the total, including the sensitivity reruns, from the first completed presentation and **stops for
the candidate** if the projection exceeds 4.0 CPU-hours rather than silently breaching C5. <!-- C3.153 --> If a
model needs more compute than the budget allows, the search grid shrinks and never the seed count
or the bootstrap resamples, because variance reporting is a gate condition and a larger grid is not.

Before any result is believed, a verification list runs: the population and completeness tables are
regenerated and must match; no feature matrix, score vector or similarity matrix may contain an
undefined value; the nine leakage assertions (L1 to L9) must all execute and report zero, with a provenance
record for every fitted object and every preprocessing constant; the split's own exclusion count
must be zero and the realised test and validation fractions must fall in their recorded ranges;
every model's split hashes must match; the cold-start visible list must be provably sorted by day;
**each of the eight fitted quantities of the refit condition must provably contain no cold-start
learner**; the test set must have been scored exactly once per model; and the phase 1 configuration
must reproduce its six recorded Precision@10 values to within 1e-6 or the difference must be
explained before any phase 2 number is quoted. <!-- C3.154 -->

OULAD is public, anonymised, secondary data, used under the CC BY 4.0 licence quoted in
`methodology/data_statement.md` from the authors' own deposit with the URL and the date it was
opened. <!-- C3.155 --> No human participants are recruited, no new data is collected and no intervention is
administered. **No attempt is made to re-identify any learner**, and no linkage with any other
dataset is performed. All reporting is aggregate — per model, per slice, per presentation — and no
individual learner's history, score or demographic combination appears anywhere in this thesis. No
demographic attribute is used as a model feature, for the reasons given in 3.3, and a fairness
caveat applies even so: the evaluated population is skewed towards highly active learners, so a
model measured on it is measured on the learners who need help least. <!-- C3.156 -->

What this data may not be used for is stated explicitly: no claim about an individual learner; no
causal claim about learning, retention or attainment; no deployment decision at another institution
without re-validation on its own data; and no redistribution of the tables from this project.

Two ethics-adjacent items remain open and are recorded rather than assumed. Whether the university
requires an ethics submission for secondary analysis of public anonymised data is **unverified**,
because the thesis regulations are not available; and the required wording, placement and scope of
the AI-assistance disclosure are likewise **unverified** and follow those regulations when the
candidate supplies them. `methodology/data_statement.md` §§6–7 carry both as open questions.

## 3.11 Recorded scope reductions and open questions

Three scope reductions are recorded in this chapter rather than left implicit: **dwell time**, which
the dataset does not record and for which click volume, active days and click sequence stand in
(3.3.1); the **user interface** promised in the proposal, deferred with its reason (3.6); and the
**deep models** named in the proposal — recurrent, convolutional and radial-basis-function networks
— likewise deferred and listed as future work (3.6). None of the three changes the hypothesis,
which remains frozen. <!-- C3.157 -->

Eight questions are open for the candidate and are carried in `methodology/protocol.md` §17 (OQ1 to
OQ8) until answered. <!-- C3.158 --> OQ1: the download URL of the local dataset copy, which is the one unverified
link in the licence provenance chain. <!-- C3.159 --> OQ2: the university's regulations for format, language, citation
style, ethics wording and AI disclosure. <!-- C3.160 --> OQ3: whether demographic attributes may be used as features
at all. <!-- C3.161 --> OQ4: the supervisor's confirmation of the two deferred scope-outs. <!-- C3.162 --> OQ5: the Arabic rendering
of *precision* as **الضبط**. <!-- C3.163 --> OQ6: whether criterion C3's two accuracy conjuncts should stay judged in
each of the 22 presentations, as the plan freezes them and this methodology runs them, or be relaxed to
the median-and-significance rule of C1 and C2, a change to what a frozen criterion demands and
therefore the candidate's decision, not the methodology's. <!-- C3.164 --> OQ7: the evaluated population is narrowed
twice, by the filter of at least five distinct resources and by the filter of at least three distinct
first-interaction days, which together remove 2,016 of the 29,228 learners in the logs; the second

Module D stays descriptive. <!-- C3.166 -->

## 3.12 What this chapter established, and what chapter 4 needs from it

This chapter fixed a design in which every compared model sees byte-identical splits, time-ordered
strictly **within** each learner though not across the cohort — a residual the chapter states
rather than leaves for an examiner; a cold-start condition in which *every* fitted quantity, not
only the similarity model, is genuinely rebuilt without the learners it calls new, each removal
carrying an assertion that emits a count, and reported beside the optimistic construction it
replaces so the difference between them is measured rather than argued, in chapter 5's Table 5.14;
a baseline set that
includes the popularity model without which the idea file's second criterion could not be judged at
all; a blend weight specified closely enough that two implementers would build the same object; a
metric set whose primary member is stated with the ceiling that binds it for between 14.4% and
81.3% of learners; and a significance plan whose families, units, corrections, tie rules,
intervals and seeds were all declared before a single run. <!-- C3.167 --> It also recorded what the design cannot
do: it cannot speak about learning, about disengaged learners — including the 997 excluded for
recording activity on fewer than three days — or about institutions other than the one the data
came from. <!-- C3.168 --> Its click weights and its candidate sets are both bounded to what a learner had
actually done at the moment each recommendation is scored, and both bounds are asserted by counted
checks rather than assumed. <!-- C3.169 --> The full list of deviations from the approved plan, D3 to D14, is in
`methodology/protocol.md` section 16, and the eight open questions for the candidate, OQ1 to OQ8, are in
its section 17. <!-- C3.170 -->

Chapter 4 needs five things from this chapter and takes nothing else: the split and cold-start
constructions of 3.5, which the implementation must realise exactly and whose assertions it must
execute and log with their measured counts; the six model specifications, the blend-weight
specification and the tuning parity rule of 3.6, with every fitted value coming from validation
data only; the metric definitions and aggregation rule of 3.7; the reproducibility and budget
record of 3.9, with its statement of the two code trees and the prefix hash, against which the run
receipts are checked; and the post-hoc additions declared in 3.8, which decide no criterion. Any departure from this chapter during
implementation is a recorded deviation, not a silent improvement.

## 6.0 What this chapter establishes

This chapter answers each research question of chapter 1 in its own words, restates the
contributions against the evidence that supports them and no further, draws the practical
implications for e-learning platforms that follow from what was measured, and sets out the future
work the analysis and the candidate's recorded decisions leave open. It takes everything it uses
from chapter 5: the six criterion verdicts of 5.5, the central finding of 5.4, the generalisation
evidence of 5.6, the post-hoc status of the diagnostics in 5.8 and the twelve limitations of 5.11.
It adds no new number, no new comparison and no new interpretation. It closes by stating what
would count as evidence against its own conclusions, because a conclusion whose refutation cannot
be described is not a scientific conclusion.

## 6.1 Answers to the research questions

### RQ0 — does the hybrid produce more accurate and more robust recommendations than either single technique, and on cold-start learners?

**Partly, and the two halves of the question have opposite answers.**
On engaged warm-user slices the hybrid is the more accurate ranker against both single techniques: a median relative gain of +31.20% over content-based filtering and +10.68% over item-based collaborative filtering across the 22 module presentations, both Holm-corrected significant, which is criterion C1 met as written (chapter 5, section 5.2 and section 5.5). <!-- C6.1 -->
On cold-start learners it is not.
Under the refit condition the hybrid beats collaborative filtering by a median +7.32% and loses to content-based filtering by a median −30.28%, both at a Holm-corrected p of 0.000241, so the claim of superiority over *both* baselines on new learners is refuted, and it is refuted on the threshold rather than for want of statistical power (section 5.3, section 5.4). <!-- C6.2 -->
The half of that comparison the hybrid wins should not be read as a cold-start success: on the same slice only content-based filtering is clearly above a random floor (+0.0310 Precision@10, 16 of 22 presentations, p_Holm = 0.011014), the hybrid is indistinguishable from that floor (−0.0038, 10 of 22, p = 0.750238), and collaborative filtering sits below it (−0.0126, 6 of 22, p = 0.046160 uncorrected and 0.092320 after Holm correction), so the baseline the hybrid beats does not itself clear chance (section 5.3.2, exploratory and deciding no criterion). <!-- C6.39 -->
On "more robust" the honest answer is weaker still: the warm-user advantage over content-based filtering reverses sign under the secondary global-cut-off split, from +31.20% to −3.77%, so robustness across evaluation designs is not established even where accuracy is (section 5.6.2). <!-- C6.3 -->
The hybrid is, however, never degenerate: it beats the global popularity baseline in 22 of 22 presentations on cold start, produces no empty top-10 list anywhere and covers at least 12% of the catalogue in every presentation (section 5.3.1). <!-- C6.4 -->

### RQ1 — which observable behaviours carry recommendation signal?

**The question has two halves. The first is answered descriptively; the second is not established.**
The behavioural signal available in this dataset is a click-volume, active-day and day-order proxy,
because the dataset records no dwell time.

*Which behaviours separate learners: answered for three of the four named families.* RQ1 names click volume, active-day count, day-ordered sequence and activity type; Module D, the descriptive analysis that ran inside the receipted main run, computed learner-level features for the first three only. It shows that click volume, distinct items, active days and distinct first-opening days separate the 27,212 evaluated learners widely — within a typical presentation the median 95th percentile is 42.8 times the median 5th percentile for click volume, 23.6 times for active days and 8.1 times for distinct items — and that a four-way segmentation is obtainable in 22 of 22 presentations (section 5.1.4). Activity type, the fourth family, was never computed as a learner-level Module D feature and so is unexamined as a learner behaviour; it appears in this thesis only as a content-side input to the content-based model and as a diversity metric (section 5.1.4). <!-- C6.43, C6.47 -->
Its separation is weak to moderate rather than clean: the silhouette coefficient has a median of 0.3274 across the 22 presentations, 18 of 22 values falling between 0.30 and 0.40 and 4 below 0.30, on a k that was fixed at four rather than selected, and the segments' leading axis is simply how much a learner did, monotonic in click volume by construction and in distinct items in 18 of 22 presentations but in clicks per active day in only 4. <!-- C6.44 -->

*Whether they carry recommendation signal: not established.* No output of that module reaches any
scored model, and its features are computed over the whole presentation with no time bound, so they
describe learners in hindsight and could not serve as a model input for new learners without leaking
the test period. The **only** measured link between behavioural features and ranking quality in this
thesis is the post-hoc D2 ablation, and this answer rests on it alone and says so: removing the visible-item count, the log click total and the active-day count from the blend's own feature set costs a median 0.001088 Precision@10 on warm users (−0.65% relative, p = 0.003279, lower in 15 of 22 presentations) and nothing detectable on cold start (median −0.000141, p = 0.674223), both small beside the gaps the criteria turn on (section 5.8.2). <!-- C6.5 -->
That measures the blend's *weighting* use of three columns, not a behavioural recommender component.

So the defensible answer to RQ1 is: three of the four named behaviour families — click volume,
active-day count and day-ordered sequence, the last covered only by two scalar summaries (distinct
first-opening day count and mean gap between new items), not by an ordered-sequence model <!-- C5.132 --> —
separate learners well enough to describe them, activity
type was not examined as a learner behaviour, and no model in this thesis ranked on the three that
were, so whether they carry recommendation signal is
unestablished — with the single exception of a small, consistent, statistically detectable
contribution to *how two other rankers are weighted* for engaged learners, and none for new
learners. <!-- C6.45 -->

### RQ2 — does segmenting learners by behavioural profile improve the cold-start prior?

**This question cannot be answered from this design, and is reported as unanswered rather than
answered by proxy.**
The plan has a behavioural module supplying a segmented cold-start prior to the hybrid, but the protocol that was pre-registered and run compares six models of which none is segmented, and chapter 3 section 3.1 records that the behavioural module is descriptive only and feeds no model; the discrepancy was recorded for the candidate before the run rather than discovered afterwards (section 5.11, limitation 7). <!-- C6.6 -->
Answering RQ2 requires a design that this thesis did not run, and it is listed in section 6.4.

### RQ3 — does a validation-fitted blend weight close the warm-user gap to pure collaborative filtering that the pilot's hand-picked weight left open?

**Yes under the design that decides the criteria, and no under the pilot's own design, and the
difference between those two answers is itself the result.**
The phase 1 pilot's hand-picked weight left the hybrid trailing collaborative filtering on warm users by a median −2.66% relative, winning in 3 of 22 presentations by at most +0.91%, while the phase 2 validation-fitted weight leads by a median +10.68%, winning in 17 of 22, with a Holm-corrected p of 0.014095 (section 5.2, section 5.4.1). <!-- C6.7 -->
That comparison is not a controlled contrast of the two weights: the phase 2 design also changed
the split, the cold-start construction and the exclusion rules, so the improvement cannot be
attributed to the learned weight alone.
Under the pilot's own random split, re-run inside phase 2 and labelled temporally leaky, the phase 2 hybrid still trails collaborative filtering on warm users by −3.57% (section 5.6.2). <!-- C6.8 -->
The defensible answer is therefore that the gap closes under a time-based split with a
validation-fitted weight, and that how much of the closure the weight itself contributes was not
isolated by any run in this thesis.

### RQ4 — does the advantage survive significance testing across the 22 presentations?

**On warm users yes, on cold start against collaborative filtering yes, and against content-based
filtering there is no advantage to survive.**
The two warm comparisons give Holm-corrected exact Wilcoxon p-values of 0.011857 and 0.014095 at an effective n of 22, and the two cold-start comparisons give 0.000241 each, the second of them on a negative median (section 5.2, section 5.3). <!-- C6.9 -->
Two qualifications belong in the answer and not after it.
The warm margin over collaborative filtering clears its pre-registered threshold by 0.68 percentage points, and the companion sign test disagrees with the pre-registered Wilcoxon on the warm comparison against content-based filtering (p = 0.052479 against p = 0.005929), the pre-registered test governing (section 5.2). <!-- C6.10 -->
And the 22 presentations are 7 modules, so these tests are indicative of generalisation and are not a random sample of courses (section 5.11, limitation 4). <!-- C6.11 -->

## 6.2 Contributions, restated against the evidence

Each contribution below is stated at the strength the evidence of chapter 5 supports, and no
further.

1. **A measured, receipted negative result on the cold-start claim.**
   The thesis shows, on a public dataset across 22 module presentations with pre-registered criteria and corrected significance testing, that a linear score-level behaviour-weighted hybrid loses to content-based filtering on new learners by a median −30.28% while beating collaborative filtering by a median +7.32% (section 5.4). <!-- C6.12 -->
   This is the thesis's principal empirical contribution, and it is a negative one for the
   hypothesis as written.
2. **A measured test of what decides the direction of the cold-start inversion.**
   All six recorded phase 1 Precision@10 values were reproduced with an absolute difference of exactly 0.0 against a 1e-06 tolerance, on EEE_2014J, the only presentation reproduced; under the primary split, the receipted run inverts the pilot's reported pattern in both directions (section 5.4.1). A further contrast (Table 5.14) holds the cold-start construction fixed and varies the split, and holds the split fixed and varies the construction: changing the split reverses the sign of the hybrid's median gain against both baselines under either construction, while changing the construction changes the sign of the median gain against neither baseline on either split, moving the median gain against collaborative filtering from +7.32% under the refit condition to +13.34% under the pilot's construction — a difference between medians only: the paired per-presentation shift has a median of about +0.01 percentage points (11 up, 11 down) and can be large in a single presentation (for example AAA_2014J, about −53 percentage points). <!-- C6.13 -->
   The inversion holds in the median over the 22 presentations (20 of 22 against CBF, 17 of 22
   against CF, 3 ties, 2 losses) and is receipted for its arithmetic on EEE_2014J only; its
   direction tracks the evaluation split, not which cold-start construction is used, and why the
   split matters is not established by any receipted cell in this thesis.
3. **An evaluation design for this problem that another researcher can re-run.** A per-learner
   time-based split, a cold-start slice in which every fitted quantity is rebuilt without the
   learners it calls new, a popularity baseline and a random floor, pre-registered criteria with a
   declared test unit, Holm correction within declared families, and per-presentation results
   published in full for all 22 presentations rather than a best one (chapters 3 and 5). Its
   verification is the criterion C4 result: zero rows shared between splits, zero of 2,992 hyperparameter records fitted outside validation, and a maximum test-scoring count of exactly 1 over 7,920 cells. <!-- C6.14 -->
4. **Evidence where chapter 2 found none, bounded by the sources it opened.** Among the sources
   opened for the literature review, none evaluated a behaviour-aware hybrid with a ranking metric
   on public learner clickstream data with a cold-start slice built by refitting without the
   held-out learners and replication across course presentations; this thesis supplies that
   evidence, and the evidence is partly negative (section 5.9). <!-- C6.15 -->

Four things are **not** claimed as contributions, because nothing here supports them: any result
about deep sequence, convolutional or radial-basis-function models, none of which was run; any
result about a behavioural recommender component, since no such component exists in this design and
the hypothesis's behavioural-signal clause remains `unchecked`; any result about learning,
retention or attainment, which this design cannot measure; and any user-facing artefact, since the
interface the proposal promised is a deferred scope-out. <!-- C6.16 -->

## 6.3 The hypothesis

The frozen hypothesis is **partially supported**, in the precise sense the criteria give that word.

Its warm-user half is supported: the hybrid is more accurate than both content-based and collaborative filtering on the warm-user slices of all 22 presentations in the median, by +31.20% and +10.68% relative, with both Holm-corrected tests below 0.05 (C1 met). <!-- C6.17 -->
Its cold-start half is refuted against content-based filtering and upheld only against collaborative filtering: C2 is not met because it requires both baselines and the content-based comparison has a median of −30.28%, and C3 is not met because its floor against content-based filtering holds in 2 of 22 presentations where the criterion requires all 22. <!-- C6.18 -->
The word "upheld" is carried at its exact weight: the collaborative baseline it is upheld against sits below a random floor on that slice, the hybrid is indistinguishable from that floor, and the popularity conjunct C3 does clear is a floor that sits a median 0.0264 Precision@10 below the random ranker, so the cold-start half of the hypothesis has no evidence of competence behind it in either direction (section 5.3.2). <!-- C6.40 -->
Its behavioural-signal clause is neither supported nor refuted: the dataset records no dwell time,
no criterion tested the clause, and the one diagnostic that touches behavioural columns tests the
blend's weighting rather than a behavioural recommender component, so the clause stays
`unchecked` and is never written as passed. <!-- C6.19 -->
Its robustness claim is not established, because the warm-user advantage over content-based
filtering reverses sign under the secondary split. <!-- C6.20 -->
No threshold was moved, in either direction, at any point after the run.

## 6.4 Practical implications for e-learning platforms

These follow from what was measured on this population and no further; each names the section that
shows it.

**For learners with substantial history, a validation-fitted blend is a reasonable default.**
On the warm slices the hybrid's median Precision@10 of 0.1720 was above all four other baselines and above the random floor's 0.0679, and it was never below both of its own components in any presentation (section 5.2). <!-- C6.21 -->
The practical form of that is the design chapter 3 specifies: fit the weight on a validation split,
never on the test data, which criterion C4 audits.

**For new learners, do not assume that a hybrid dominates.** In 20 of 22 presentations
content-based filtering alone ranked better for cold-start learners than the hybrid did, and the
hybrid sat between its two components in 19 of 22 (sections 5.3 and 5.8.1). <!-- C6.22 -->
A platform that faces genuine cold start has, on this evidence, no reason to prefer an even blend
over its content-based component at that moment. Whether a content-leaning fixed weight would do
better was deliberately not tested here, so the implication stops at "do not assume" and does not
become a recommended weight.

**A popularity fallback is not what the hybrid degenerates to, and absolute cold-start quality is
low.** The hybrid beat the global popularity baseline on cold start in 22 of 22 presentations by a
median +76.46%, so it is not a disguised popularity ranker; but the popularity baseline it clears sits a median 0.0264 Precision@10 below a random ranker on that slice, so clearing it is close to unavoidable for any ranker with signal, and the hybrid's own cold-start median of 0.0473 is indistinguishable from the random floor's 0.0484 (10 of 22 wins, p = 0.750238) (section 5.3.2, exploratory, deciding no criterion). <!-- C6.23 -->
The practical implication is blunt and is the one a platform should act on: for genuinely new learners this hybrid, in this design, gives no measurable benefit over ranking at random, while content-based filtering alone does (+0.0310 Precision@10 over the floor, 16 of 22, p_Holm = 0.011014). <!-- C6.41 -->
Any deployment decision on new learners has to be judged against the attainable ceiling of 0.6482 to 0.9435 and against that floor, not against the raw number. <!-- C5.12 -->

**For anyone evaluating a recommender, within these receipted cells, the choice of evaluation split can decide the conclusion, more than the cold-start construction does.** Holding the cold-start construction fixed, moving from a per-learner time-based split to the pilot's random 20% split reverses the median direction of the hybrid's cold-start comparison against both baselines; holding the split fixed, moving between the corrected refit construction and the pilot's shared-similarity construction reverses the median direction of neither comparison, though individual presentations do change sign on that move (for example FFF_2013J and FFF_2014J against CF on the primary split, AAA_2013J against CF on the tertiary split) (section 5.4.1, Table 5.14). <!-- C6.24 -->
That is a consistent practical finding within these receipted cells, and it costs nothing to apply.

**Nothing here is an implication about learning.** Clicks record that a resource was opened, not
that it was read, understood or useful, and no number in this thesis supports a claim about
attainment or retention (section 5.10).

## 6.5 Future work

Drawn from the analysis above and from the candidate's recorded decisions, in the order a
continuation would take them.

1. **A genuine behavioural recommender component** — a sequence or session model that ranks on the
   day-ordered interaction sequence rather than three learner-level activity columns feeding a
   blend weight. This is the only way to test the hypothesis's behavioural-signal clause, which
   remains `unchecked` (section 5.5). Such a component would also have to compute its features under
   a time bound, which the descriptive features of section 5.1.4 do not have: they are read over the
   whole presentation and so could not be fed to a model for a new learner without leaking the test
   period. <!-- C6.46 --> It is a **new model and a new pre-registration**, not a
   follow-up run on the closed criteria of this thesis, and its criteria must be fixed before it
   runs. <!-- C6.25 -->
2. **The deep models and the user interface the proposal promised.** Recurrent, convolutional and
   radial-basis-function networks and the user-facing interface are deferred scope-outs by the
   candidate's recorded decision, stated in chapter 1 with their reason — a single-CPU compute bound
   and a later phase reserved for deep learning — and they remain the proposal's open commitments,
   pending the supervisor's confirmation that deferring them is acceptable. <!-- C6.26 -->
3. **An evaluation on actual new learners, and on other data.** Cold start here is simulated by
   truncating an evaluated learner's history to k items, and the learners closest to real new users
   — the 997 whose whole recorded activity falls on one or two days — were removed by the day filter
   (section 5.11, limitations 1 and 2). A design that measures those learners, and a replication on
   a second dataset or a second institution, are what would let any of these conclusions travel
   beyond one UK distance-learning cohort. <!-- C6.27 -->
4. **The fixed-alpha question.** Whether any fixed blend weight would have beaten content-based
   filtering on cold start was judged boundable and test-safe but knob-turning on a closed
   criterion, and was excluded (section 5.11, limitation 9). As a pre-registered question in its own
   study, with its own held-out data, it is a legitimate and cheap next experiment. <!-- C6.28 -->
5. **Confirming or refuting D1's reading.** That the hybrid lands between its components because a
   roughly even fitted weight is placed on the weaker one is a correlate, not a mechanism; testing
   it means manipulating the weight and re-measuring, which this thesis may not do to a closed
   criterion but a new pre-registered study may (section 5.8.1). <!-- C6.29 -->
6. **The segmentation question RQ2 asks.** A design in which a behavioural segmentation actually
   supplies the cold-start prior, compared against the non-segmented hybrid on the same splits,
   would answer the question the plan poses and this run could not (section 5.11, limitation 7). <!-- C6.30 -->
7. **A cold-start design that clears the floor at all.** Section 5.3.2 shows that at k = 2 visible items the hybrid is indistinguishable from a random ranker and that only content-based filtering is clearly above it, so the open engineering question is not how to weight two components on new learners but whether any of the rankers available here helps them; reporting every model against a random floor should be a standing requirement of that work rather than a post-hoc check. <!-- C6.42 -->
8. **The evaluation-design question the secondary split raises.** The warm advantage over
   content-based filtering reverses sign under a global temporal cut-off, so which of the two split
   constructions better represents a deployed setting is an open methodological question that
   affects how strongly C1 may be read (section 5.6.2). <!-- C6.31 -->

## 6.6 What would count as evidence against these conclusions

Stated so that a reader can check the conclusions rather than accept them.

- **Against the central finding:** a run under this protocol, on these 22 presentations, in which
  the hybrid's cold-start Precision@10 has a positive median relative gain over content-based
  filtering with a Holm-corrected p below 0.05. The measured value is a median −30.28% with 20 losses of 22, so the contradicting evidence would have to move a large and consistent gap, not a marginal one. <!-- C6.32 -->
- **Against the finding that the inversion tracks the split rather than the cold-start
  construction:** a contrast, on the primary or the tertiary split, in which moving from Condition A
  to Condition B changes the sign of the hybrid's **median** gain against CBF or against CF. Table 5.14 runs
  exactly that contrast on both splits and finds no such sign change in the median (section 5.4.1);
  individual presentations do cross zero on that move (primary, vs. CF: FFF_2013J, FFF_2014J;
  tertiary, vs. CF: AAA_2013J), which is why the condition is stated at the median and not the
  per-presentation level. A future run that found a sign change in the median would overturn this
  reading, not confirm it. <!-- C6.33 -->
- **Against the warm-user conclusion:** evidence that the global temporal cut-off is the more appropriate evaluation design, which would put a −3.77% median in place of +31.20% against content-based filtering; or a demonstration that the warm result does not hold once ceiling-bound learners, who are 14.4% to 81.3% of the evaluated learners depending on the presentation, are handled differently. <!-- C6.34 -->
- **Against the reproducibility claims:** any of the 52 receipted artifacts failing to re-hash to
  its recorded value, or a re-run of the phase 1 reproduction differing from the recorded values by
  more than 1e-06. <!-- C6.35 -->
- **Against the cold-start reading of section 5.3.2:** a pre-registered comparison in which the hybrid clears a random floor on a cold-start slice, since the measured value is a median −0.0038 Precision@10 with 10 of 22 wins and p = 0.750238; a resampled rather than five-seed-averaged floor, or a floor computed per comparison, could also change that reading. <!-- C6.39 -->
- **Against the generalisation:** a result on an actual new-learner population, or on a second
  institution or dataset, with a different ordering of the three models on cold start; or evidence
  that the stratified cold-start cohort draw is not comparable with the warm cohort it is drawn to
  match. <!-- C6.36 -->
- **Against the gap claim of chapter 2:** a published study that evaluates a behaviour-aware hybrid
  with a ranking metric on public learner clickstream data with a refit cold-start slice and
  replication across course presentations. Two candidate items were refused by their publishers
  during the review and read by title only, and they are the first place to look. <!-- C6.37 -->
- **Against any of it:** a defect found by re-executing the code.
  Five such defects were found by execution during this project, four of which aborted every run and one of which was a design question the engineer was not permitted to settle alone (section 5.7.3). <!-- C6.38 -->

## 6.7 What this chapter established

This chapter established the answers this thesis can defend: that the hybrid recommender is more
accurate than both single-technique baselines for engaged learners and is not more accurate than
content-based filtering for new learners, where it is in addition indistinguishable from a random
floor, so the hypothesis is partially supported, its warm half holding, its cold-start half refuted
against one baseline and upheld only against a baseline that is itself below chance, and its
behavioural-signal clause unchecked; that the median direction of the cold-start comparison against each baseline depends on the evaluation
split rather than on which cold-start construction is used, which is a consistent finding
within these receipted cells;
that one research question the plan poses cannot be answered from this design; and that the
contributions are a receipted negative result, a measured evaluation-design effect, a re-runnable
design and a filled gap in the sources opened. It established no conclusion that section 5 did not
measure, and it names for each conclusion the evidence that would overturn it.

What remains for the candidate rather than for this chapter: the university's regulations, which
fix the language of each part, the format, the citation style and the disclosure wording; the
supervisor's sign-off on the two deferred scope-outs and on the five discrepancies chapter 2 found
between the approved proposal and the papers it summarises; and the two questions that would change
what a criterion demands, whether C3's accuracy floors stay per-presentation and whether the
cold-start criterion is to be read as requiring actual rather than simulated new learners. None of
them is decided here.

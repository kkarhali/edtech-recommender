# Threats to validity

Companion to `methodology/protocol.md` (unit `u07_s3_methodology`, cycle 0, written 2026-09-21).
Every row carries either a mitigation that exists somewhere in the protocol, with its section, or a
residual limitation written in the words chapter 3 and chapter 5 will use. A row with neither is an
open item, not a finished row. Rows T1 to T16 are the project's pre-recorded threats, corrected
against the design now specified; T17 to T28 are introduced by that design or discovered while
writing it, or added in answer to a G3 verdict: T22 rewritten and T25, T26 added at revision 1;
T22 rewritten again and T27, T28 added at revision 2; T27 rewritten and T29 added at revision 3.

| # | Threat | Type | Mitigation in the design | Residual limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | The pilot's random 20% holdout ignores `date`, so training can include interactions later than the test items | Internal | Primary split is the per-learner **day-boundary** split of protocol §5.1, with the strict per-learner assertion L1; the random split is kept only as the tertiary comparison and is labelled temporally leaky wherever it appears (§5.3) | **A per-learner split is leakage-free within a learner, not across the cohort.** Every learner shares the presentation's calendar, so the item-item similarity, the popularity counts and the blend model are fitted on other learners' training and validation pairs that can postdate a given learner's test items. The global cut-off split of §5.2 is the robustness check that removes this, at the cost of dropping late-joining learners. This residual is now carried in chapter 3 (§3.5.1) as well as here, and §5.3a pre-declares that the primary split governs a criterion verdict even if the secondary reverses a sign |
| T2 | Item similarity or learned factors fitted on all rows including test | Internal | Similarity, popularity counts and every learned quantity are built from training pairs only, asserted by L2 and visible in the run log (§5.4) | None if the assertion executes. An assertion that is not executed is `unchecked`, never `passed` (rule 6), so the log line must exist even when the count is zero |
| T3 | The pilot's iteration 2 blend weight was chosen after iteration 1's test results were seen | Internal | The blend weight is produced by a logistic regression fitted on the validation split only (§8, §11); C4 asserts that no hyperparameter is fitted outside validation and that test is scored once | The pilot's numbers cannot be quoted as a tuned-and-validated result; they appear in chapter 4 labelled exploratory. The validation-fitted `α` is still the one object in the design that has seen validation data, which is why C4 audits it specifically |
| T4 | `NaN` week features made the hybrid exactly equal content-based filtering in BBB_2013B, DDD_2013B and DDD_2013J | Internal | Verified: `week_from`/`week_to` are unknown for 100.0% of items in exactly those three presentations, and nearly so in DDD_2014B (0.2% known) and DDD_2014J (0.3%). P5 falls back to `0.0` when a column has no known value, a `week_missing` indicator is always a feature, and §13 asserts that no feature matrix, score vector or similarity matrix contains `NaN` or `inf` — an assertion, not only a `nan_to_num` safety net | The pre-fix numbers stay in the record with their root cause (rule 4). Content features carry almost no week information in five of the 22 presentations, so CBF there is effectively an `activity_type` model and its results are reported separately |
| T5 | Selecting the module presentation could favour a result | Internal | The deterministic rule (interaction count closest to 400,000, ties by name) is pre-registered and picks EEE_2014J at 402,947, with BBB_2013B the nearest competitor at 403,266 (§4, P1); the main run covers all 22, so the choice affects only the C6 reproduction | The deterministic rule is still one arbitrary criterion; it is defensible because it is fixed before any result, not because 400,000 is meaningful |
| T6 | One dataset, one country, one institution type | External | All 22 presentations are run and published; the population and its limit are stated in `data_statement.md` and repeated in chapters 3 and 5 | Results may not transfer to the candidate's own institution, to on-campus learners or to a non-UK cohort. Nothing in this thesis licenses a deployment decision elsewhere without re-validation |
| T7 | Classical similarity models only; no learned collaborative or sequence model | External | Phase 2 adds a validation-fitted learned blend (§8); the deep models (RNN, CNN, RBFNN) are deferred scope-outs by the human's recorded decision Q5, stated in chapter 1 and listed as future work in chapter 6 | Conclusions about a hybrid's ceiling hold for **linear score-level blending of cosine CF and cosine CBF**, not for hybrids in general, and not for any neural architecture |
| T8 | Clicks are a proxy for learning need | Construct | Stated in the design paragraph (§1) and repeated in the limitations; engagement metrics are reported as engagement and never as attainment | No claim about learning, retention or attainment follows from any number here. The design cannot support a causal statement and the thesis does not make one |
| T9 | Unobserved items counted as negatives | Construct | Full-catalogue ranking with no sampled negatives (§7); catalogue coverage and intra-list diversity reported (§9) | Exposure is missing-not-at-random: the log was produced by the VLE's own navigation, so an item a learner never opened may be an unobserved positive. This remains unmodelled |
| T10 | Precision@10 is ceiling-bound for learners with fewer than 10 test items | Construct | Quantified per presentation in §9.1, recomputed under the §5.1 split as corrected at revision 2: **45.6%** of EEE_2014J's evaluated learners are ceiling-bound, and the share ranges from **14.4%** (FFF_2013B) to **81.3%** (GGG_2014B). Recall@K, NDCG@K, HitRate@K and MRR@K are reported at K = 5, 10, 20, and the distribution of test-set sizes is published beside every Precision table | The primary criterion stays on a ceiling-bound metric because `plan.md` fixed it (rule 3). A Precision@10 difference **between presentations** is never read as a difference in model quality |
| T11 | Differences of a few percent between models | Conclusion | Paired Wilcoxon per learner within a presentation with a rank-biserial effect size, bootstrap 95% intervals over learners (10,000 resamples), and the deciding test over the 22 presentations (§10.2, §10.3) | A statistically detectable gap may be practically negligible; chapter 5 reports the effect size beside every p-value and says both. The pilot's median gap against CF was −2.7% |
| T12 | Many pairwise comparisons across models, slices, metrics and k values | Conclusion | Families F1 to F4 are declared before the run and Holm correction is applied within each; F4 is labelled exploratory and supports no criterion (§10.1) | Exploratory comparisons remain exploratory in the thesis text; none may be promoted to evidence for a criterion after the fact |
| T13 | The 22 presentations share courses and content | Conclusion | Per-presentation results published in full, plus a per-module breakdown over the 7 modules so a reader can see whether a result is driven by one module (§10.4) | The 22 observations are not independent: they are 22 presentations of 7 modules. The across-22 tests are indicative of generalisation, not a random sample of courses |
| T14 | The pilot's cold-start condition is scored by a similarity matrix fitted on the cold-start learners' own training history, so they shaped the model that ranks for them | Internal | Condition A refits the similarity model with the cold-start learners' rows removed from the matrix itself and decides C2 and C3; Condition B is kept for continuity, labelled "simulated thin history (optimistic)" everywhere it appears, and decides nothing; the difference between them is reported as the **measured** optimism (§6.1 to §6.3) | No test interaction ever leaked, which is why this defect survives a leakage check. The residual point is interpretive and now explicit: a thin-history result cannot be read as a new-learner result, and the phase 1 cold-start numbers are thin-history numbers |
| T15 | The hypothesis names dwell time, which OULAD does not record | Construct | Verified: `studentVle.csv` carries `code_module`, `code_presentation`, `id_student`, `id_site`, `date`, `sum_click` and no duration field anywhere. Summed `sum_click`, distinct active days and the day-ordered sequence are declared as the proxy in chapters 1 and 3 as a recorded scope reduction (human decision Q2); the hypothesis stays frozen (§3.2) | A click count cannot distinguish a resource opened once by accident from one studied for an hour. The behavioural signal in this thesis is thinner than the hypothesis describes, and the word "dwell time" is never used for anything that was measured |
| T16 | The evaluated population is the very active one | External | Verified for EEE_2014J: 1,188 registered, 1,097 with any VLE activity, **1,018 evaluated** after both filters, the log-present learners holding a mean of 44.8 and a median of 49 distinct resources of a 115-item catalogue; the two filters together remove only 79 of 1,097, so the course and platform produce the skew, not the filters. Across the 22, 29,228 learners appear in the logs and **27,212** are evaluated (§4.1). The interaction distribution is reported beside every headline metric and the item filter is repeated at 3 and 10 as a sensitivity run (§4, §12.1) | The disengaged learners a recommender is meant to reach are largely absent before measurement begins — 91 registered learners in EEE_2014J never touch the VLE at all — so no result in this thesis speaks for them |
| T17 | `studentVle.csv` is not one row per learner, resource and day, although `OULAD.names` describes it that way | Internal | Verified: EEE_2014J's 402,947 rows collapse to 303,660 distinct (`id_student`, `id_site`, `date`) triples, 74,972 of which carry more than one row, up to 10; across the file 1,614,505 of 8,459,320 triples are duplicated. §4 therefore defines every click total as an explicit `sum` over a group and every active-day count as a count of **distinct** `date` values (§2.2, defect 2) | Why the duplication exists is not documented by the dataset's authors, so the protocol handles it deterministically rather than explaining it. A reader who counts rows instead of pairs will not reproduce these numbers |
| T18 | The dataset's own documentation miscounts its students, and a project artifact carries a third figure | Internal | Verified: `OULAD.names` §5 states 32,953 students and 32,953 registration entries; `studentInfo.csv` and `studentRegistration.csv` each hold 32,593 data rows and `OULAD.names`'s own §9 totals 32,593. Four other figures in the same §5 check out exactly. Every count in this thesis is taken from the CSV that holds it and names that file (§2.2, defect 1) | The discrepancy is the dataset's, not this thesis's, and is stated rather than silently corrected. A reader comparing against published OULAD papers may meet the 32,953 figure |
| T19 | The cold-start cohort could be systematically different from the warm cohort, which would confound C2 | Internal | The cohort is a seeded (42) 20% draw **stratified by quintile of the learner's training-pair count**, so it matches the warm cohort's activity distribution; the two distributions are reported side by side. The more literal "latest-arriving 20% by first interaction date" construction is run as a robustness check at k = 2 (§6.1) | Neither construction is a real new registration. The stratified draw controls the confound but is not literally "new"; the time-ordered cohort is literally new but may be systematically thinner. Both are reported and chapter 5 says which one each claim rests on |
| T20 | The licence provenance chain has one unverified link | Internal / integrity | CC BY 4.0 is quoted in `data_statement.md` from the dataset authors' own figshare deposit (DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.5081998.v1, opened 2026-09-21) and corroborated by the UCI mirror; the original OU page now redirects to a page with no licence statement, and the local archive contains no licence file (8 members, verified) | The bytes analysed were extracted from `oulad.zip` whose fetch URL is recorded nowhere, so the licence is verified for *the dataset* but not traced to *this copy*. Recorded as open question OQ1 and stated in chapter 3 rather than hidden |
| T21 | The global temporal cut-off split changes the evaluated population | Internal | The cut-off day D is the 80th percentile of the presentation's interaction days, fixed per presentation in §5.2 **before** the run so it cannot be chosen on results; the number of learners who lose their training history under it is reported per presentation | The secondary split answers a different question about a partly different population. Where it disagrees with the primary split on the sign of a comparison, chapter 5 says so and suppresses neither |
| T22 | **The day-based split requires three active days, and the learners it excludes are not a random sample** | Internal / External | Two rules were withdrawn after measurement, each recorded rather than quietly replaced. Revision 0's tie-extension rule emptied train/validation for 1,345 of 28,209 learners (4.8%) and inflated the test set 1.08-1.26x. Revision 1's day-boundary rule let the test cut fall on the second day, which left 106 of 27,212 learners with no validation set although they passed the stated filter — the published tables dropped them silently while asserting an exclusion count of zero. Revision 2 bounds the test cut at the third distinct day (§5.1 step 2), which makes three active days **sufficient** as well as necessary: verified 0 learners with an empty slice and 0 L1 violations across all 27,212, so the filter and the split now describe the same learners and the zero-exclusion assertion is true. Realised fractions (test 0.199-0.205, validation 0.102-0.120) are reported per presentation, and P4b has its own sensitivity rerun at a threshold of 2 (§12.1) | **997 of 28,209 learners (3.53%) are excluded by P4b, and they are a distinct group**: a mean of 10.0 and a median of 8 distinct resources across a mean of 2.66 and a median of **2** active days, none above 14 days, against 55 resources over 52 days for the 27,212 retained (counted 2026-09-21). They are learners whose whole recorded activity falls on one or two days — the closest thing in this data to `idea.md` criterion 2's "actual new users". No result in this thesis speaks for them; chapter 3 §3.4 says so and OQ7 puts the narrowing in front of the supervisor |
| T27 | **The candidate exclusion set can encode the learner's future, and did** | Internal | §7.1 fixes one principle — exclude exactly what the learner has opened *as of that score's prediction moment* — over **three** moments: warm validation (training only), warm test (training ∪ validation) and cold start at either boundary (the visible `k` pairs). Assertion L8 checks all three and emits the count of learners failing each. Revision 1 excluded training ∪ validation in *all* conditions; measured on EEE_2014J at k = 2, that inflated cold-start Precision@10 from 0.0255 to 0.2157 for `MostPop` (8.5x) and from 0.0333 to 0.1946 for item-kNN CF (5.8x), and converged the two to within 10.8% where the corrected rule leaves CF 31% above `MostPop`. Revision 2 then applied the test-boundary set at the *validation* boundary, putting the ground truth inside its own exclusion set and scoring validation Precision@10 at 0.0000 against 0.1883 (300 warm EEE_2014J learners, `MostPop`), which would have made the warm blend weight, `N` and `C` degenerate | The adopted rule is the honest one, but it makes cold-start Precision@10 genuinely small (0.025 to 0.033 on EEE_2014J), so C2 and C3 turn on narrow absolute margins and chapter 5 must argue from effect sizes and intervals, not point estimates. Two successive revisions got the exclusion set wrong in opposite directions, which is why L8 now checks every moment rather than every condition |
| T29 | **The warm blend model's features shift between fitting and scoring** | Internal | A consequence of tying the exclusion set to the prediction moment: the warm candidate set at test time (M2) is smaller than at validation time (M1) by the learner's validation items, so `cf_top1`, `cf_top10_mass`, `cbf_top1` and `cbf_top10_mass` differ slightly between the rows the blend model was fitted on and the rows it scores. §8.1 step 4 discloses it and the run log reports the mean absolute shift in each of the four features per presentation | The shift is not removable: eliminating it would mean using one exclusion set at both moments, which is exactly the degeneracy that made validation Precision@10 zero. It is small and measured rather than assumed, and it does not arise in the cold conditions, where one information state serves both boundaries |
| T28 | **Ranking ties are broken by an arbitrary rule that is not neutral in effect** | Conclusion | §8.0 rule (i) sorts by score descending then `id_site` ascending, a total order that is pre-registered, identical across all six models and independent of any model's score, so it cannot favour one model over another. This matters because at k of 0, 1 and 2 ties spanning the tenth place are the normal case, and at k = 0 every personalised model degenerates to the `id_site` order | The tie-break still decides which of several equally-scored resources is recommended, so cold-start Precision@10 carries a component that is an artefact of resource numbering rather than of any model. It is identical across models, so comparisons are unaffected; absolute cold-start values are not, and chapter 5 says so |
| T25 | The blend model is fitted where visible history is the full training set and applied at k = 2, far outside that range | Internal | §8.1 step 11 **refits** the `α` model for Condition A on the 80% warm learners with visible history truncated to the same k, so one `α` model exists per (presentation, condition, k) and none is extrapolating | If the refit model still differs sharply from the warm one, the hybrid's cold-start result is partly a statement about blend specification rather than about the architecture; chapter 5 must report the fitted `α` distributions per k and say which |
| T26 | A frozen-criterion test can be undecidable rather than failed | Conclusion | §10.2a fixes the zero-difference rule (`zero_method="wilcox"`, ties dropped, effective n reported) and the zero-baseline rule (undefined ratios excluded from the median, with the exclusion count reported). Below an effective n of 6 the exact two-sided test cannot reach p < 0.05, so the result is reported as `inconclusive (effective n)` and recorded `unchecked` under rule 6 | Bit-identical model outputs are known to occur here — the pilot produced them in BBB_2013B, DDD_2013B and DDD_2013J — so a criterion's test conjunct may end the study `unchecked` rather than passed or failed, and chapter 5 must present that as the third outcome it is |
| T23 | Excluding already-seen items defines a task different from next-click prediction | Construct | §7 states the task in one sentence and names the exclusion and its reason; the pair-level split guarantees an item is never in both a learner's training and test set, so exclusion never removes a ground-truth item | Learners legitimately revisit resources, and this design cannot say anything about the usefulness of recommending a revisit. Results are not comparable with next-click sequence papers that include seen items |
| T24 | Adding baselines and metrics beyond `plan.md` could be read as moving the goalposts | Conclusion / integrity | `Random` and `PopWeek` are additions recorded as deviations D4, and the added ranking metrics as D5; **none decides any criterion**. C3's "global-popularity baseline" remains `MostPop` alone, and C1 to C3 stay judged on Precision@10 exactly as `plan.md` states (§9, §16) | Chapter 5 must keep the criterion verdicts visually separate from the supporting tables, so no reader can mistake a favourable added metric for a criterion that passed |

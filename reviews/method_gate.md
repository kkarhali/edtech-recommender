## Notes

- **Deciding unit (22 presentations) is defensible.** The plan leaves the Wilcoxon unit unstated; fixing it at the presentation keeps the criterion's median and its test on one unit, and n=22 is the less powerful reading than per-learner n of 350 to 2,196, so D3 does not loosen the bar. The non-independence of 22 presentations from 7 modules is disclosed and a per-module breakdown promised (`protocol.md:587-593`). The sign test companion is correct (exact binomial, two-sided, on the signed differences) apart from the zero-difference rule (W2). D3 is a substance-neutral operationalisation, so no escalation to the human is needed.
- **C3 aggregation stands as a named choice and OQ6 is correctly a human decision.** The methodologist did not relax C3(a) and (b); replacing "in each of the 22" with median plus significance would let a hybrid that falls below CBF or MostPop in one or two presentations pass, which changes what a frozen criterion demands (rule 3, decision reserved for the human). The split of C3 into structural invariants (empty lists, coverage), for which a universal quantifier is the only sound aggregation, and accuracy floors, which the frozen text and the stricter reading keep universal, is coherent, and reporting the conjuncts additionally in C1/C2 style inside F4 adds information without deciding anything. Manager decision M4 expected the methodologist to "align" the aggregation; the methodologist correctly took the second route offered in the G1 verdict (record it as a deliberate choice), which is what the human's OQ6 now needs. One residual for chapter 5: C3 compares point estimates with no uncertainty, so a tie or a noise-level shortfall in one of 22 presentations fails the criterion; the per-presentation bootstrap intervals the protocol already promises should be shown beside each C3 verdict.
- **Non-degeneracy conjunct.** `idea.md:23-24` also asks for lists "not identical for every user". C3 has no such conjunct, but coverage >= 10% covers it: identical top-10 lists give 10 divided by the catalogue, at most 9.43% (catalogue minimum 106), which fails the 10% bar in all 22 presentations. This holds only if "available items" means the catalogue size; worth saying so.
- **L1, L4, L5, L6 are executable as written** (strict inequality with the tie rule making it provable per learner, pair-set intersections, SHA256 equality, a scoring counter). L2 and L3 are not (B3).
- **Licence claim.** The claim "CC BY 4.0 at the authors' own deposit" is supported by what is quoted: a figshare API license field naming the same three authors as the data descriptor, corroborated by a second host, with the licence text and its attribution duty quoted; the claim is carefully limited to "the dataset as deposited". The remaining gap (fetch URL of the local `oulad.zip` unrecorded, no licence file in the archive) is honestly stated in `data_statement.md:79-86`, `protocol.md:377` (OQ1), `thesis/ch03.md:105-110` and threat T20. I verified from the local files that the zip has exactly the eight members listed and that `OULAD.names` contains no match for licen, creative commons, CC-BY, copyright or terms. I did not re-fetch the web sources, so the quotation itself remains the methodologist's evidence, not mine. This is not a blocker.
- **Chapter 3 versus protocol.** I found nothing in `thesis/ch03.md` that the protocol does not do, and no number in it that fails to reproduce except those that inherit B1 (3.4 item 4 is fine, 3.7.1 is not). Two omissions rather than overclaims: the cross-learner leakage residual of T1 (W1) and the missing statement of the alpha model's target (B2). The claim at 3.12 that every model sees "strictly time-ordered splits" is true per learner only.
- **Design consequence to watch, not a defect.** The alpha model is fitted where visible history is the whole training set (tens of items) and applied to cold-start learners at k=2, out of its training range. A hybrid that loses to CF at cold start could therefore be showing a mis-specified blend rather than a limit of the architecture. Chapter 5 should say which, and the protocol may wish to state that the alpha model is refit on the 80% learners with visible history truncated to k, if that is the intent.

## Overall

Decision: revise. The dataset facts reproduce exactly (hashes, 32,593 rows, the duplicated triples, the week-field completeness), the criteria are copied verbatim, the C3 asymmetry is correctly kept and OQ6 correctly left to the human, and the ethics and licence statements are honest about their gaps. The strongest reason for not approving is that the protocol's own pre-registered ceiling figures (47.9% for EEE_2014J and the whole 9.1 table) reproduce only on a split without the tie rule the same protocol mandates; with the rule, 4.8% of evaluated learners lose their training set and the ceiling share falls to 38.1%. Two further defects, an undefined blend-weight fit and a cold-start refit whose exclusion of held-out learners is asserted only for the similarity matrix, would each let an examiner re-run the design and reach a different model or a contaminated cold-start result. Fixing B1 to B3 (and disclosing W1 in chapter 3) would change this to approve; none of the fixes touches a threshold or needs the human.

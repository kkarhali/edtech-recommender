## Blockers (must fix before proceeding)

- [B1] <severity:high> `methodology/protocol.md:702-707` (7.1, "Consistency with the blend model"), `protocol.md:797-806` (8.1 step 2 and step 4), `protocol.md:818-819` (step 8), `protocol.md:778-788` (8.0a), `protocol.md:486` (L8): the single exclusion principle is applied at the **wrong prediction moment** in the warm condition, so every fitted quantity that is scored on validation items degenerates. Steps 2, 4 and 8 and 8.0a score the label, the `N` selection and the `C` cross-validation on the learner's **validation items** "using that condition's exclusion set from section 7.1", and 7.1 fixes the warm exclusion set at **training union validation**. The ground truth of a validation-time score is a subset of the exclusion set, so no validation item can be recommended and Precision@10 is 0 by construction. I measured it: EEE_2014J, 300 warm learners, `MostPop`, exclusion = training union validation, Precision@10 on validation items = **0.0000, max 0.0**; the same learners with exclusion = training only score 0.1883. Consequences as written: (i) every warm learner's Precision@10 is 0 at every alpha, so every learner is dropped as "all-zero" (step 2), fewer than 20 labelled learners remain, and step 10's degenerate rule sets alpha to the constant `median{alpha*(u)}` = 0.5 in every presentation, so the warm hybrid is a fixed 50/50 blend and C1 would be judged on a model that is not the one specified; (ii) `N` is a tie across the whole grid and the "smallest N" rule picks 10 in every presentation; (iii) step 8's cross-validation scores every `C` at 0 and picks the smallest. Nothing would abort: L8 is satisfied and the run would emit a plausible table. This is the same principle the revision states, applied at the wrong moment: at validation time the prediction moment is the validation boundary, so the learner has opened only the training pairs (warm) or only the visible k pairs (cold; the cold case is correct as written). It bites only the warm condition (C1 and the warm slice of F3). Rule=2 (a number must trace to the rule that produces it) and rule=6 (the fit is `unchecked`; measured it is degenerate). Required: state that the exclusion set is defined per prediction moment: for any score whose ground truth is the validation set (label construction in step 2, `N` selection in 8.0a, the fold scores in step 8, and the feature construction at validation time in step 4) the warm exclusion set is the **training pairs only**, and training union validation applies only at the test boundary; extend L8 to assert both moments; state in step 4 that this makes the warm candidate set differ by the validation items between validation time and test time (a small, disclosed shift in `cf_top1` and the top-10 mass features, see W6). No number in the tables of sections 4.1 and 9.1 changes. | impact: without it the warm hybrid, the selected `N` and `C`, and therefore C1 are fitted on an all-zero target and the deciding result is silently degenerate.
- [B2] <severity:high> `methodology/protocol.md:247` (definition of `clicks(u, i)`) read with `protocol.md:735-736` (`ItemKNN-CF` uses the click matrix; `CBF` profile is click-weighted), `protocol.md:823` (feature `log1p(total visible clicks)`), `protocol.md:499-502` and 6.4 (visible history), 5.4 L1: `clicks(u, i)` is the sum of `sum_click` over **all rows** of the pair, with no date bound, while `first_date` alone places the pair in training, validation or test. A pair is therefore in the training or visible set by its first day, but its click weight includes every later revisit, including revisits after the test boundary and, for a cold learner, everything after the k-th interaction. This is a path by which a learner's future reaches the fitted and scored quantities, and L1 cannot detect it because L1 tests `first_date` only. Measured on EEE_2014J (1,018 learners, section 5.1 split): warm, **17.8%** of the clicks on training pairs are dated on or after the learner's test boundary, and 1,014 of 1,018 learners are affected; cold k = 2, **96.9%** of the clicks carried by the two visible pairs are dated after the day of the second interaction, and 1,015 of 1,018 learners are affected. The item-item matrix (CF and the CF half of the hybrid), the CBF profile weights, and the blend feature `log1p(total visible clicks)` all read those weights, so a new learner's "two prior interactions" carry a weighting that encodes that learner's later engagement. The leak is asymmetric across the models compared: `MostPop` counts distinct learners and does not read clicks, so C3(b) (hybrid above `MostPop`) and the CBF/CF/hybrid comparison of C2 are biased toward the click-weighted models. It does not reveal which items were later opened, so it is smaller than the exclusion oracle of cycle 1; it still means the cold-start condition is not "<= 2 prior interactions" in the sense `idea.md` criterion 2 uses, and the warm split is not leakage-free within a learner as L1 claims. Rule=2, rule=3 (the criterion-2 population would be measured on a slice that carries later information) and rule=6 (L1 passes while the leak is present). Required: define the click weight as of the prediction moment, for example `clicks(u, i | t)` = the sum of `sum_click` over rows dated **before the learner's first validation day** for every model's training use (so validation-time scoring is also clean), and over rows dated **on or before the k-th visible pair's `first_date`** for the visible history in every cold-start condition; state that `n_active_days` in step 3 counts distinct dates of rows inside the same bound; add a counted assertion (call it L9) that emits, per learner and per fitted or scored object, the number of rows dated after the bound that were read, required 0; and re-run the `ItemKNN-CF` line of the 7.1 probe table, which used the all-rows definition (the `MostPop` line does not read clicks and stands). None of sections 4.1, 5.1 or 9.1 changes, because they depend on `first_date` only. | impact: as specified, the cold-start condition that decides C2 and C3 and the warm condition that decides C1 score click-weighted models on weights that already encode the learner's future, in a way the protocol's own leakage assertions would report as clean.

## Warnings (should fix)

- [W1] <severity:med> `methodology/protocol.md:517-520` (6.1 visible history), 6.4, A8: the text says "the earliest k items by `first_date`" without saying the pool is the learner's **training** pairs. Where a learner has fewer than k training pairs, an implementer who sorts all pairs would let a validation or test pair become "visible" (and, under the new exclusion rule, excluded from candidates, removing a ground-truth item). Measured over the 22 presentations: 168 of 27,212 evaluated learners have fewer than 2 training pairs and 761 have fewer than 5 (EEE_2014J cold cohort: 0 below 2, 7 below 5), so it is a small share at the deciding k = 2 and larger at k = 5, which decides nothing. State that the visible pool is the training pairs, that the visible count is `min(k, training size)`, and assert `visible subset of training`.
- [W2] <severity:med> `methodology/protocol.md:611-622` (6.1a, the independent hash check): the check is sound against a function that reads a cold learner's row through its argument, but the claim that "a function that silently read a cold learner's row cannot pass this, whatever it reported" is too strong. A function that reads a module-level frame instead of its `allowed` argument reads the same global in both builds and returns equal hashes. Make the rebuild run in a **fresh process** on a data file from which the cold rows were physically removed, and hash inside that process; then the claim is true. The seven hash pairs should also be defined over a canonical serialisation (sorted, fixed dtype) so equality does not depend on row order. Without the fix the check is still a useful second line, which is why this is a warning.
- [W3] <severity:low> `methodology/protocol.md:734-735` (`PopWeek`) and 8.0(i): `PopWeek` takes the week from "the learner's last training `first_date`", which in the cold conditions is the learner's own future (it should be the last **visible** pair's date), and items outside the window are not ranked, so the top-10 can be shorter than 10 and the order over the whole candidate list is not total. Both matter only because `PopWeek` decides nothing (D4), but the rule "a total order for every model" is otherwise true and should be true here too: append the ineligible items after the eligible ones in `MostPop` order, and fix the date to the visible history in cold conditions.
- [W4] <severity:low> `methodology/protocol.md:750-754` (8.0(i)): the order is total over the candidate set only if score equality is defined. CF scores are float sums, so two mathematically equal scores can differ in the last bit depending on summation order. State the summation order (for example by `id_site` ascending) or round scores to a fixed number of decimals before ordering. The protocol otherwise gives a total order for all six models: `MostPop`, `Random` (seeded per learner and candidate), CBF, CF (with the in-neighbourhood tie rule) and the hybrid (a constant at k = 0 by the stated zero-vector rule).
- [W5] <severity:low> Stale cross-references, cosmetic but an examiner will find them: `protocol.md:755` says the tie-break is recorded in "threat T27", but T27 is the exclusion-set threat and the tie threat is T28 (`threats_to_validity.md:36`); `threats_to_validity.md:33` (T21) still says `D` is the 80th percentile of "interaction days" while 5.2 fixes it as a percentile over interaction **rows**; the opening bullet of section 7 and T23 still describe the split as "pair-level" although 5.1 is a whole-day split (the conclusion, that an item is never in both training and test, still holds).
- [W6] <severity:low> `methodology/protocol.md:806-812` and B1's fix: once the exclusion set is defined per moment, the warm hybrid's features at validation time (exclusion = training) and at test time (exclusion = training plus validation) come from candidate sets that differ by the validation items, so `cf_top1` and `cf_top10_mass` shift slightly. That is a correct consequence of "as of the moment", not a defect; say so in step 4 and report the shift once, so the sentence "features are built exactly as the ones the model was fitted on" is not left standing unqualified.
- [W7] <severity:med> Design of C2 and C3 at cold start (a finding for chapter 5, not a blocker). I estimated the noise of the deciding number: EEE_2014J, k = 2, `MostPop`, cohort n = 204: mean Precision@10 0.0255, per-learner SD 0.059, **81% of cold learners score exactly 0**, standard error 0.0041 (16% of the mean); at the smallest cohort (69) the standard error is about 0.0071 (28%). (a) C2 is decided by the median relative gain and a Wilcoxon over 22 presentation-level means, which averages this noise across 22 cohorts, so C2 is decidable and the design is fair to it. (b) C3(a) and C3(b) are frozen as per-presentation strict comparisons in **all 22**, with no noise allowance; a true tie in quality will fail in several presentations by chance, and this reflects a choice already sent to the human as OQ6, not an error. The protocol should therefore print, beside each per-presentation C3 verdict, the paired bootstrap interval of the difference (the machinery exists, 10.2) and pre-declare that a within-interval "not met" is still reported as "not met". (c) Because the target of a cold learner is the items first opened after the boundary, items the learner opens in the middle of their history count as misses for every model, which is why 0.0255 is far below the ceiling of section 9.1; the ceiling table is computed for the warm slice, and a cold-start ceiling (the same table with the visible-only exclusion) should be added so 0.02 to 0.03 is not read as failure of the models. The choice is identical across models and biases no comparison.

## Notes

- **Question 1 (the numbers).** All reproduce, listed above. The population table is now the population the rule states, and the split needs no unstated removal.
- **Question 2 (is the single exclusion principle applied consistently).** The principle itself is sound and is applied correctly in three of the four places I was asked about. Test time (both conditions) and cold-start validation time: correct. Condition B: correct (exclusion = visible k, refit per k with all learners including the cold ones, which is what makes it optimistic). The N selection in 8.0a: correct for the cold conditions (visible history truncated to k, exclusion = visible k, `N` re-selected per k, hybrid inherits it). It is **not** correctly applied to the warm validation-time computations (alpha label, `N`, `C`): the text applies the test-boundary set at the validation boundary (B1). The alpha-label construction in the cold conditions is consistent, because label and test-time features share the same exclusion rule and only the ground truth differs.
- **Question 3 (future information reaching a fitted quantity or a candidate exclusion).** I traced every learner-derived object of 6.1a. No test item reaches a label, a feature, a standardiser, a fold score, `N` or `C`; test items appear only as unlabelled candidates at validation time. No cold-cohort learner reaches any Condition A object, by construction of `W`. Cohort membership itself is stratified by the cold learners' training-pair count, which selects who is cold but is not a fitted quantity. One path remains open: the click weights (B2). Two minor ones are W1 (visible pool) and W3 (`PopWeek` date, reporting only).
- **Question 4 (6.1a independent hash check).** Sound in design and worth keeping, with one qualification: it must run in a fresh process on a physically reduced data file for the stated guarantee to hold (W2). It covers cold-cohort rows, not the click-date bound of B2, which needs its own assertion (L9).
- **Question 5 (total order).** Yes for all six models: score descending then `id_site` ascending, `Random` scored by a seeded draw, a stated zero-vector rule for constant vectors, a stated in-neighbourhood tie rule for CF, so ties at the tenth place resolve identically for every implementer. Two qualifications: float equality (W4) and `PopWeek`'s ineligible items (W3), both minor and the second affecting a model that decides nothing.
- **Question 6 (fairness of C2 and C3).** See W7. C2 is decidable at n = 22; C3's per-presentation floors are noisy by construction, which is the frozen criterion and OQ6, not a design defect.
- **Cycle 0 and cycle 1 items.** Cycle 1's B1 (population versus split) and B2 (exclusion oracle) are repaired and reproduce. B3 (tie-breaks, constant-vector min-max, `N` selection) is repaired in 8.0 and 8.0a apart from the warm validation-time exclusion (B1 above). Cycle 1 warnings W1 to W5 are addressed in the text (all-zero learners dropped and reported, validation slices for the secondary and tertiary splits, the seven-object count, the reproducible cohort draw, D11 and D12 labelled).
- **OQ7, who decides.** Adding the two learner filters (>= 5 items, >= 3 days; 1,019 + 997 = 2,016 of 29,228 learners, 6.9%) is an **operationalisation the methodologist may adopt**, not a human-only decision and not a rule 3 or decision-1 matter. Rule 3 governs criteria moved after a run; standards decision 1 reserves the research question, hypotheses and success thresholds. The filters change none of the hypothesis text, the six criteria, their thresholds or their `on_dataset` fields (checked programmatically), and `idea.md` criterion 2 explicitly admits "simulated" new users, which the cohort construction supplies for the evaluated learners. What the human alone can decide is the **reading** of criterion 2: if the candidate wants "actual" new users (learners with one or two real visits), the filter design is in question and needs a redesign. The protocol already carries that as OQ7 for the supervisor, with the limitation in T22, ch03 and the sensitivity reruns; nothing further blocks on it.
- **Convergence.** Two blockers, both with small, local fixes that touch no threshold, criterion or hypothesis and no number in sections 4.1, 5.1 or 9.1. The methodologist has one revision left. Under a disciplined reading I believe **one further revision is needed** and **should be sufficient**: B1 is a wording and assertion change in 7.1, 8.0a, 8.1 (steps 2, 4, 8) and L8; B2 is a definition change in section 4 plus one new assertion L9 and the re-run of the `ItemKNN-CF` probe line. ## Overall

Decision: revise. Everything that made cycle 1 a revise is genuinely fixed and reproduces: the population, the split, the ceiling table, the cohort sizes, the cold-start exclusion (0.0255 against 0.2157 reproduced), the tie, scaling and `N`-selection rules. Two new blockers remain. The exclusion principle is applied at the test boundary where validation-time scoring needs the validation boundary, which makes the warm alpha label, `N` and `C` fit on an all-zero target (measured 0.0 on 300 learners); and the click weights carry post-boundary engagement (17.8% of training clicks warm, 96.9% of the visible clicks at k = 2 in EEE_2014J) that the first-date assertion L1 cannot see, and that `MostPop` alone is immune to. Fixing both as described, with the `ItemKNN-CF` probe line re-run and no other number changed, would move this to approve at cycle 3.

## Blockers (must fix before proceeding)

- [B1] <severity:high> `methodology/protocol.md:341-350` (5.1 steps 2-4), `protocol.md:260, 295` (P4b and the population table), `protocol.md:1075` (D11), `methodology/threats_to_validity.md:33` (T22), `thesis/ch03.md:203-219, 292-295`, `methodology/data_statement.md:105`: the split rule and the evaluated population do not describe the same learners. Step 2 lets the test cut `j*` fall anywhere in `2..m`. When `j* = 2`, step 3's validation range `2..j*-1` is empty, so the training or validation set is empty although the learner has >= 3 distinct first-interaction days. Recomputed, this happens to 106 of 27,212 learners who pass P4a and P4b as stated (0.39%, up to 16 of 1,404 in BBB_2013B); they are the learners whose first day holds roughly four fifths or more of their pairs. Every number the protocol publishes (1,103 dropped, 27,106 evaluated, EEE_2014J 1,017, all 22 rows of section 4.1, the realised fractions, the whole 9.1 ceiling table, cohort sizes) reproduces **only** if those 106 are also removed, and no sentence says so. As written, step 4's "the code asserts that the exclusion count at this step is 0" would fail on the 106 and abort the run. The T22 and ch03 description of the excluded group ("opened many resources in one or two sittings and did not return") is false for these 106, who hold a median of 5 active days. Rule=2 (a number enters the thesis only if the rule that produces it is the rule written) and rule=6 (the claim "the split's own exclusion count is zero" is `unchecked`, never `passed`; measured, it is 106). The fix is small (for example require `j* >= 3` in step 2, or state the filter as "the split is constructible") but every downstream number changes with it and must be regenerated. | impact: the pre-registered population, the ceiling table and the T22 limitation would all be re-derived differently by a second implementer, and the deviation D11 records a filter that is not the one applied.
- [B2] <severity:high> `methodology/protocol.md:564-575` (section 7 exclusion set) read with `protocol.md:477-481, 484-487` (6.1 visible history, k = 2) and `protocol.md:510-511` (A7, A8), `thesis/ch03.md:399-407`: in Condition A the exclusion set is the learner's **whole** training and validation history while the visible history is the earliest k items. A learner who is "new" at k = 2 therefore has about 33 further training items and their validation items (EEE_2014J mean 33.1 training pairs, 115-item catalogue) removed from every model's candidate list, which uses knowledge of the learner's behaviour after item k that a real new learner would not have supplied. Cycle 0's W3 asked whether validation items are excluded; the answer given ("cannot bias a comparison because it is applied to all six models") does not consider the cold-start condition. ## Overall

Decision: revise. The revision fixed what cycle 0 raised: the tie-extension split is gone, the blend fit is specified down to solver and output mapping with no test item or cold learner able to enter it, the fitted quantities of Condition A are tabled with counted assertions, and no threshold, criterion or hypothesis text moved. It also introduced a new design (whole-day split, a days filter, an exclusion set shared by the cold-start condition), and that design fails on three counts. The published population reproduces only if 106 learners the split cannot serve are also removed, which no sentence says (B1). The cold-start condition removes each new learner's whole later history from the candidates, which changes `MostPop`'s cold-start Precision@10 from 0.023 to 0.203 in a one-presentation probe and so is not a new-learner test (B2). And tie-breaking, constant-vector scaling and the `N` selection history at cold start are still unstated, so C2 and C3 could be rebuilt two ways (B3). Fixing B1 to B3, with every number regenerated, would change this to approve; none of the fixes touches a threshold, and none needs the human, though the filter narrowing should be shown to the supervisor.
